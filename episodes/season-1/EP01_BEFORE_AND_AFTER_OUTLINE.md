# Season 1, Episode 1 — Before and After

Status: DRAFT OUTLINE / expanded working draft
Updated: 2026-10-08

## Story window

**7–30 September 2009**

Length: **long-form; no fixed word-count target**.

The episode should feel broad rather than rushed. It covers almost a month, but routine days are compressed.
The reader should experience several complete "windows" into Nathan's life and understand what happens
between them.

## Central movement

**Nathan already has a life. The move does not create him; it displaces him.**

The episode begins on his thirteenth birthday before school starts, with computing, football and an
established social/academic identity. His father's promotion then abruptly redirects the family's future.
The new school introduces Alex and a second social world. The episode closes with Nathan no longer entirely
new, but still unfinished in both his new life and his game.

## Persistent threads

### A — Nathan / game
Prototype → A* baseline → stress test → bottleneck → flow-field adaptation → first integrated field movement.

### B — Old school
Teachers and teammates already know Nathan. The reader sees why through interactions rather than exposition.

### C — Football
Nathan is already captain and comfortable inside an established team.

### D — Pierce family
Birthday → promotion → logistics → relocation → temporary rental setup.

### E — New school
New kid → teacher reactions → classmates → first foothold.

### F — Nathan + Alex
Recognition → observation → avoidance → Alex initiates → repeated contact.

### G — Modern Family AU
Pilot on 23 Sep and The Bicycle Thief on 30 Sep, shown in parallel unless Nathan has a genuine reason to
intersect them.

### H — Living background
Shopping, packing, household setup, teammates, teachers, family members' independent plans, objects acquiring
provenance.

### I — Pre-Pilot family contact lines
Four people in the Dunphy orbit become familiar with Nathan before **23 September** without forcing him into
the Pilot: **Claire (16 Sep), Phil (17 Sep), Haley (19 Sep), Alex (16–22 Sep)**.

Detailed contact logic is kept separately in:
episodes/season-1/EP01_PREPILOT_CONTACT_THREADS.md.

## MOVEMENT I — 7–13 SEPTEMBER
### Old life before the move

### 7 September — birthday / opening image

**Calendar:** Labor Day. No ordinary school day. citeturn275803search0turn275803search1

#### Scene 1 — Nathan at the computer
Open quietly with action rather than explaining reincarnation.

Nathan is thirteen today.

He is working on the game. The machine is already configured for C++ development, but the **game itself begins here**. There is no
assumption that meaningful development happened before the opening scene.

He starts with:
- a basic project/window;
- a minimal tile/grid representation;
- player movement;
- one deliberately simple enemy;
- the first navigation experiment.

He is not trying to invent an unusual algorithm immediately. He starts with the conventional solution and lets
measurement expose the problem.

Character beats:
- he notices a small inefficiency before anyone else would care;
- refuses to call the prototype "good";
- thinks about the problem mathematically;
- has a notebook with sketches rather than polished design documents.

The reader should infer advanced competence from the way he works.

#### Scene 2 — birthday breakfast
Clifford and Heather celebrate him.

Keep it domestic and ordinary.

Useful characterization:
- Nathan tries to say he does not need much;
- parents know he always says that;
- a gift connected to football/computing can show they understand his interests;
- he is more comfortable receiving practical gifts than emotional praise.

Do not make the birthday a monologue about destiny.

#### Scene 3 — football
A holiday training session, pickup session or organized youth practice is plausible.

Nathan is already captain.

Show the contrast:
- in computing he works alone and overthinks;
- once teammates talk to him, he jokes easily;
- he makes a tactical suggestion;
- a teammate follows it without drama.

One teammate, **potential recurring peer**, is introduced as someone who trusts Nathan but also wants more
playing time.

#### Scene 4 — quiet family undercurrent
A very small domestic detail:
- Clifford takes a work call;
- Heather notices the tone of the conversation;
- Nathan notices but does not know what it means.

This plants the promotion before the reveal.

#### Scene 5 — evening coding
Nathan runs the prototype.

One guard = fine.

Five guards = fine.

He decides to try more.

The benchmark is not introduced as exposition. We see the number on his screen increase.

End beat:
**"Why are five different guards all spending time finding essentially the same road?"**

### 8 September — old school begins

#### Scene 6 — mathematics
Nathan arrives at school already comfortable with the material.

Teacher gives a problem.

Nathan solves it quickly.

Teacher asks him to explain it.

Nathan:
- starts explaining at too high a level;
- realizes it;
- simplifies;
- worries he has sounded arrogant.

Teacher does not praise him as a genius. Instead:
- asks one harder follow-up;
- notices he can actually explain the reasoning;
- quietly decides he may need harder work later.

This teacher should be a possible recurring academic relationship.

#### Scene 7 — lunch / classmates
A classmate starts a conversation.

Nathan is normal once spoken to.

The reader sees that he is **not socially awkward in conversation**.

His problem is initiation.

#### Scene 8 — football / captaincy
Another short beat:
- teammate asks whether they should change formation;
- Nathan answers;
- everyone follows.

Later:
someone compliments the decision.

Nathan credits the team's execution.

This establishes leadership without self-confidence.

#### Scene 9 — game
At home, Nathan adds more guards.

The bottleneck becomes clearer.

He uses a profiler.

Important visual/narrative point:
the computer is doing work Nathan considers wasteful.

Do not explain profiling extensively; show him staring at the same operation repeating.

---

### 9 September — contrast with California

#### Scene 10 — school
Science/lab.

Nathan proposes a cleaner experiment.

Teacher makes him justify why.

He does so calmly.

His classmates' reaction should be modest:
"Okay, that actually makes sense."

#### Scene 11 — football
Practice includes a small conflict:
- one teammate wants more time on the field;
- Nathan mediates practically;
- he does not give the teammate a motivational speech;
- he identifies what the player needs to improve.

This teammate can later become important.

#### Scene 12 — evening / Clifford's news approaches
Clifford's work day becomes more urgent.

A short parallel family scene with Clifford and Heather establishes:
- the California role has been under discussion for weeks;
- the outcome is still uncertain;
- relocation support is already being discussed as a possibility.

This means the later "sudden" news is sudden **to Nathan**, not magically produced overnight.

---

### 10–11 September — game + first emotional disruption

#### Scene 13 — A* stress test
Nathan runs 10, 15, 20, 24 enemies.

The first serious performance degradation occurs.

He experiments with:
- fewer updates;
- cached paths;
- different destinations.

The result is better but not good enough.

He gets irritated at the program rather than himself:
the code is doing exactly what he told it to do.

#### Scene 14 — family
Clifford gets confirmation that the promotion has been approved and that the new role is California-based.

Nathan learns the news.

The scene should begin with practical questions:
- When?
- Where?
- What about school?
- What happens to football?
- What about his computer?

Then the emotional reaction arrives later.

He realizes:
- he had a team;
- teachers knew him;
- routines were established;
- he was not looking for a new life.

#### Scene 15 — parents' explanation
Clifford explains that the company had been negotiating the move quietly.

Important:
- the promotion is sudden in its final confirmation;
- the logistics are unusually fast because the company already has a relocation provider;
- they will initially rent a house;
- they will buy later after the family knows the area.

This resolves the house problem.

#### Scene 16 — Nathan alone
Nathan returns to the game instead of talking.

This is not avoidance of the move in a melodramatic way.

It is simply the one problem he can still control.

---

### 12 September — last football weekend

#### Scene 17 — match
Nathan plays his final match before leaving.

The team knows he is moving.

Keep emotions restrained.

A teammate tells him they will miss him.

Nathan immediately assumes:
"They're being nice."

This shows self-undervaluation outside romance.

#### Scene 18 — coach
Coach tells Nathan he has leadership qualities.

Nathan is embarrassed.

Coach gives him practical advice for joining another team.

Later, this advice can matter.

#### Scene 19 — moving logistics
Boxes begin appearing.

Do not turn this into a shopping documentary.

Select details:
- computer equipment carefully packed;
- books divided into "must have" and "can wait";
- football gear;
- one household object Heather realizes they need;
- moving company contact.

---

### 13 September — last quiet Sunday

#### Scene 20 — family planning
Breakfast/table scene.

Clifford and Heather discuss timing.

Nathan gets a small amount of choice:
- what he wants in his room;
- which equipment travels with him;
- what should be shipped.

This reinforces that the parents respect his independence.

#### Scene 21 — technical reading
Nathan revisits material on pathfinding and field-based navigation.

He does not suddenly remember a perfect answer.

He recognizes a technique he already studied.

His thought:
A* is solving the right problem repeatedly.
A shared field could represent the reusable part.

He makes rough notes.

#### Scene 22 — school departure preparation
Nathan sends a few messages to teammates / friends.

No huge farewell scene.

A sense that his old life is ending because of logistics rather than a dramatic decision.

## MOVEMENT II — 14–15 SEPTEMBER
### The move

### 14 September

#### Scene 23 — departure
Compressed travel sequence.

Use sensory details:
- airport;
- luggage;
- computer case;
- tired parents;
- Nathan reading rather than watching something.

#### Scene 24 — Clifford starts California role
Can be a short parallel scene.

He is already working.

This makes the promotion feel real rather than "dad got a new title."

#### Scene 25 — temporary rental
The family enters the California rental.

Important:
- it is a real house;
- it is empty enough to personalize;
- it is clearly not yet "home";
- some furniture may be temporary;
- boxes dominate the rooms.

Nathan sets up the minimum computer environment.

#### Scene 26 — local geography, not yet direct neighbors
The temporary rental is in the same broad area as the Dunphys, but it is not the permanent house next door.

Nathan notices:
- a useful route to school;
- a nearby football field/park;
- local shops;
- the difference between the new area and the family's old routine.

This establishes the geography that will make later encounters plausible without making the family instantly
interlocked.

The actual pre-Pilot contact scenes are deliberately spread across 16–19 September.

#### Scene 27 — first night in California
Nathan looks at the map / school route.

He also looks at his unfinished game.

The two problems are suddenly connected:
he is going to have to rebuild a life and finish a project simultaneously.

## MOVEMENT III — 16–23 SEPTEMBER
### First week at the new school / first contact with Alex

### 16 September — first school day

#### Scene 28 — arrival
Nathan is early.

He knows where the classrooms are only in theory.

He checks his schedule.

His new timetable differs enough from Seattle that the day feels unfamiliar.

#### Scene 29 — mathematics
New teacher.

Nathan answers carefully.

Teacher notices.

No reputation has followed him here.

This is important:
**he has to earn the new reputation from zero.**

#### Scene 30 — pickup area: Claire
After the school day, Nathan is briefly disoriented by the pickup routine.

Claire is collecting one of her children and notices that Nathan is checking the campus layout/timetable.

She asks whether he is new.

The exchange is practical:
- Nathan confirms;
- Claire gives one small local school/pickup tip;
- they introduce themselves naturally if the moment lasts;
- Nathan recognizes Claire only after the name is spoken.

Nathan becomes slightly too formal because he knows exactly who she is.

Claire's first impression is simply:
**polite, organized, a little serious.**

This is the first non-Alex bridge into the Dunphy family.

#### Scene 31 — hallway: Alex
Nathan sees Alex.

Recognition.

He knows exactly who she is.

For several seconds he considers approaching.

Then:
- too awkward;
- she is with someone;
- he does not want to bother her;
- he will have another chance.

He walks away.

This is the first visible romantic self-sabotage.

#### Scene 32 — after school
New football contact.

Nathan does not need to befriend anyone immediately. The important thing is that the new team becomes a
real place in his weekly schedule.

Nathan is useful immediately but does not dominate.

He notices the new group has different habits than his old team.

That distinction matters later.

#### Scene 32 — game
Evening coding.

Nathan's first flow-field attempt starts from his notes.

He gets the field mostly right.

Movement is fast.

Then two guards collide and behave badly.

He marks it rather than abandoning the method.

### 17 September

#### Scene 33 — school / Alex at distance
Library/academic context.

Alex is working.

Nathan notices.

He deliberately focuses on his own assignment.

A student asks Nathan a technical question.

Nathan explains naturally.

Alex may overhear part of it.

Nathan does not notice her noticing.

#### Scene 34 — English
Teacher catches Nathan over-editing a written answer.

The teacher says the answer was already good before he changed it five times.

Nathan is mildly embarrassed.

This becomes an early seed for his perfectionism.

#### Scene 35 — family evening
Claire/Phil or another family member is briefly shown elsewhere dealing with their own ordinary problem.

Nathan is not there.

The cut exists simply to establish that the Dunphy household is already alive before the Pilot.

#### Scene 36 — late-afternoon football / Phil
Nathan goes to a nearby community field for informal football work.

Phil happens to be there for an ordinary family/local reason.

Phil comments on Nathan's control or footwork.

Nathan initially thinks he is talking to somebody else.

The conversation is brief:
- football;
- recent move;
- names;
- one light Phil joke.

Nathan recognizes Phil and deliberately keeps the recognition from showing.

The important characterization is that Nathan becomes naturally conversational once Phil initiates.

Phil leaves thinking:
**new kid, good player, serious at first, probably easy enough to talk to.**

This can later support a natural Phil-as-realtor connection.

#### Scene 37 — flow-field review
Nathan opens the reference material again.

He sees the connection between his measured bottleneck and the field representation.

Technical thought:
the expensive information should be shared; the cheap differences can stay individual.

### 18 September — first Alex conversation

#### Scene 38 — practical reason
Alex needs a book/reference/resource.

She notices Nathan has it.

She asks.

Nathan knows exactly who she is.

Alex does not know that.

Conversation:
- practical;
- brief;
- mildly awkward;
- not romantic.

Nathan explains the material correctly.

He then worries that he sounded like he was correcting her.

Alex leaves thinking something closer to:
"He's weirdly helpful."

#### Scene 39 — after the conversation
Nathan replays the conversation in his head.

Not:
"She likes me."

Instead:
"She probably just needed the book."

This is the central self-undervaluation.

#### Scene 40 — evening coding
The first flow-field version works better.

Nathan smiles once.

Then notices a steering defect.

Perfect.

### 19–20 September — weekend bridge

#### Scene 41 — new football environment
Saturday activity/match.

Nathan earns respect.

The competitive teammate is introduced as a possible friend.

Nathan does not become captain.

#### Scene 42 — Haley contact / community weekend
Nathan is at the local community park/football area for a weekend activity.

Haley is nearby because of an ordinary family/social reason.

A tiny practical interaction creates the meeting:
- holding a gate;
- returning something dropped;

Haley makes a light remark, Nathan answers, and the conversation ends.

There is no romantic framing.

Haley simply learns that the new kid is Nathan.

Nathan recognizes her immediately and is quietly struck by the imbalance between what he knows and what she knows
about him.

Her first impression should be no deeper than:
**quiet, but not weird.**

#### Scene 43 — home/family adjustment
Parents unpack.

Heather begins establishing a household routine.

Clifford spends long hours working.

Nathan finds the kitchen / local street / route to school unfamiliar.

Small detail:
one item the family needs is bought for the rental.

#### Scene 44 — Nathan + game
He works on local steering.

The enemies no longer get trapped as often.

Prediction remains absent or primitive.

#### Scene 47 — Alex echo
Nathan thinks about Friday's conversation while coding.

He notices that he has been smiling.

He immediately becomes annoyed with himself for losing concentration.

#### Scene 46 — school

A teacher remembers his name.

#### Scene 45 — Alex

## Word-count architecture

Target **10k–14k+**.

A useful distribution is:

- 2,000–2,500 words: old life / birthday / school / football;
- 1,500–2,000: promotion and move;
- 2,500–3,000: new school / Alex first week;
- 2,000–2,500: game development;
- 1,500–2,000: parallel family/canon/background material.

This is a guide, not a quota. Strong scenes should expand naturally.

## Drafting warning

Do not show every day.

Use dates heavily in the underlying timeline, but let prose jump:
"By Friday..."
"Over the weekend..."
"Three days later..."

# Expanded scene architecture — causal / narrative pass

Status: WORKING ADDENDUM
Updated: 2026-10-08

This layer is deliberately richer than the final prose. It records the causal chain behind visible scenes so the
episode can later be edited into a fluid narrative rather than written as a calendar.

## Narrative camera

Nathan remains the default centre of the episode, but the camera may leave him when another character's
actions create a consequence Nathan cannot see.

Useful modes:

- Nathan-centered scene: objective action + Nathan's interpretation.
- Family cutaway: a short self-contained scene showing what the Dunphys/Pritchetts/Pierces are doing
  independently.
- Consequence cutaway: show a small decision by another character before its effect reaches Nathan.
- Canon parallel: show a canonical family thread because it is interesting in itself, not because Nathan
  needs to be present.

The reader is therefore allowed to know more than Nathan.

## Causal spine

### 7 September — birthday without school

1. Labor Day removes the normal school routine.
2. That gives Nathan a longer uninterrupted morning.
3. He spends more time with the prototype.
4. The extra test exposes the repeated-path cost earlier.
5. Clifford's work call happens in the middle of an otherwise normal birthday.
6. Nathan notices the call but cannot yet connect it to a concrete change.
7. The evening benchmark becomes the first technical and narrative pressure point.

The birthday is therefore not decorative: the unusual day gives the opening more space and plants the work
problem immediately.

### 8–13 September — established life before displacement

Nathan's old school, football team and coding routine are shown in different scenes rather than one exposition
dump.

The important causal relationship is:

**existing competence → existing social trust → move removes the context in which that competence is already
recognized.**

This is why the first new-school scenes should deliberately feel less comfortable even though Nathan has
not changed.

The competitive teammate introduced at the old school should have one concrete unresolved need (for example,
more playing time or a tactical role). Nathan handles it practically and the teammate begins to trust him.
The later farewell therefore has a specific relationship to lose.

### 10–11 September — promotion becomes a real event

The promotion should not appear as information created for the episode at the moment Nathan is told.

Backstory already exists:

- negotiations were happening before 7 September;
- Clifford knew California was a possibility;
- Heather knew enough to discuss logistics;
- the company had a relocation provider;
- the final confirmation came later.

This makes the family's rapid move believable.

A small scene from Clifford's perspective can occur before Nathan learns anything.

The reader can therefore realize that Nathan's old life is already becoming temporary before Nathan knows it.

### 12–15 September — departure

The final football match should pay off the old teammate thread.

Nathan's self-undervaluation appears here in a non-romantic form:
when someone says they will miss him, he assumes politeness before accepting that the feeling may be real.

The move then removes familiar social reinforcement.

At the temporary rental, use a restrained "first night" sequence:
the house is physically functional but psychologically unfinished.

Nathan chooses one room corner for the computer because he needs something familiar.

That choice later lets the same physical place feel gradually transformed by Episode 2.

## 16–22 September — building a social map

### 16 September

The first day should not contain every possible "new kid" beat.

Suggested sequence:

**arrival → one academic test → lunch/transition → football → pickup/Claire → evening coding**

The Alex sighting works best when it occurs during a transition rather than as an isolated romance scene.

Nathan recognizes her.

He does not approach.

Later, Claire recognizes that he is new.

These two events occur in the same day but have completely different emotional weight for Nathan.

### 17 September — revised Alex beat

Nathan does **not** interact with Alex because he plays cello.

Alex's cello remains part of her independent life, but Nathan's second sight should arise from a
normal school transition such as:

- moving between an academic classroom and the music wing;
- seeing Alex carrying the cello case while Nathan is on the way to his own activity;
- passing the music-room corridor after being sent to another classroom;
- briefly waiting in a shared corridor while Alex comes out of practice.

The point is not the instrument.

The point is that Nathan learns a second real-world detail about her that memory alone cannot provide:
what her actual school-day rhythm looks like.

No conversation is required.

### 18 September — first real Alex interaction

The book/resource exchange remains the anchor.

The hidden causal rule is:

**Nathan already owns the useful material because he genuinely studies that subject → Alex happens to need that
material → the existing possession creates the interaction.**

It must not feel like Nathan carrying a prop because the author needs Alex to speak to him.

After the scene, Nathan's interpretation should remain more negative than the reader's.

### 19 September — Haley

The weekend meeting should arise from the shared geography established by the temporary rental and
Nathan's football routine.

The exact micro-event can be selected during prose drafting according to the physical blocking of the
scene. It should not be fixed so tightly that it reads manufactured.

### 21–22 September — repetition rather than escalation

The key change is not "romance increases."

The key change is:

**first contact → recognition → remembered interaction → easier second interaction → voluntary third
interaction.**

Nathan speaking one small sentence first on 22 September is enough to show movement.

Alex does not need to think, "I like him."

She only needs to choose to keep talking.

## 23 September — Pilot without Nathan insertion

The Pilot should be allowed to breathe as its own ensemble story.

The camera may spend meaningful time away from Nathan.

A useful structural rhythm is:

**Nathan school routine → Dunphy/Pritchett canon thread → Nathan game → another canon beat → Alex/family
material → Nathan night scene**

Nathan's prior knowledge can create irony, but he should not narrate the episode to the reader.

Where remembered canon predicts a reaction, the story may deliberately show the real reaction from another
character's perspective.

## 24–30 September — consequences become more visible

The important development is the beginning of the butterfly effect.

Nathan should not immediately cause a giant divergence.

Instead, use small changes:

1. one timing difference;
2. one person now knows Nathan exists;
3. one ordinary conversation happens;
4. that person makes a slightly different decision;
5. another character encounters the result later.

A cutaway can show steps 3–4 before Nathan ever notices step 5.

### Example causal style

Claire's day changes a minor errand.

Nathan's day independently creates another errand.

The two schedules overlap.

The resulting meeting is technically a coincidence, but the reader can reconstruct why both people were
there.

That is the preferred model for later encounters.

## 24–30 September — game causality

The technical thread should visibly build from earlier measurements.

### Baseline

A* solved the immediate navigation problem but repeated the same computation too often.

### Adaptation

Nathan reviews the field-based method he already studied.

### Integration

The shared field handles broad navigation.

Local steering handles collision and movement differences.

### New problem

Navigation is not destination selection.

The player can still make choices that invalidate a purely reactive system.

### First prediction/influence layer

Nathan therefore experiments with:

- last-known position;
- recent direction;

Each step is an engineering response to an observed failure, not a sudden "genius idea."

## First-draft freedom

These are continuity constraints, not prose constraints.

During the first prose pass:

- dialogue can discover itself;
- exact micro-blocking can change;
- two short beats can become one scene;
- one planned beat can disappear;
- a stronger causal connection discovered while writing can be backpropagated into the grid.

The revision pass should then repair continuity rather than forcing the first outline to survive unchanged.
