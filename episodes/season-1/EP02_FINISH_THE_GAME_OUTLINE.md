# Season 1, Episode 2 — Finish the Game

Status: DRAFT OUTLINE / expanded working draft
Updated: 2026-10-08

## Story window

**1–15 October 2009**

Drafting references: use [the dialogue naturalness standard](../../canon/narrative/DIALOGUE_NATURALNESS_STANDARD.md), [Season 1 relationship pacing](../../canon/narrative/SEASON_1_RELATIONSHIP_PACING.md), [Maxwell's working profile](../../canon/characters/MAXWELL_WHITAKER.md), and [the foreknowledge/intervention policy](../../canon/narrative/FOREKNOWLEDGE_INTERVENTION_POLICY.md). Scene beats are constraints, not dialogue to expand mechanically; keep *The Incident* parallel unless an established, credible interaction gives Nathan a reason and a way to help.

Target finished length: **10,000–14,000+ words**.

The episode should feel like the beginning of normal life after the move. The game is the strongest through-line,
but the episode must contain multiple independent plots.

## Central movement

**Nathan is no longer trying to become settled; he is settled enough to start building something.**

The game reaches completion around the middle/later part of the episode.

The emotional plot is quieter:
Nathan and Alex become comfortable enough that their conversations no longer require an excuse.

## MOVEMENT I — 1–6 OCTOBER
### Becoming part of the routine

### 1 October

#### Scene 1 — school continuity
Do not reintroduce Nathan.

He knows the building.

He knows where to sit.

A teacher remembers the stronger answer he gave in September.

This immediately tells the reader that the previous episode happened.

#### Scene 2 — Alex
Alex starts a conversation without needing a book or assignment.

The conversation is short.

Nathan almost leaves, then stays.

This is a small victory.

#### Scene 3 — football
Maxwell asks Nathan for help on a tactical situation.

Nathan gives a useful answer.

The teammate calls him by name casually.

The social bridge is now real.

#### Scene 4 — game
Prediction field produces a destination that is plausible but too precise.

A guard appears psychic.

Nathan hates it.

He needs uncertainty.

---

### 2–3 October

#### Scene 5 — school
A teacher gives Nathan an optional harder exercise.

Nathan finishes it.

Teacher asks what he does outside school.

Nathan mentions programming.

Nothing dramatic.

Teacher stores the information.

This is the seed for a future academic opportunity.

#### Scene 6 — Alex / observation
Alex notices Nathan reacts strangely when praised.

She asks why.

He gives an evasive answer.

Alex does not push.

This is important because she is beginning to observe him.

#### Scene 7 — family
Short Pierce-family domestic sequence:
- boxes are mostly gone;
- kitchen routine is established;
- Melissa knows where things are;
- David is working longer hours.

The temporary rental begins to have ordinary life.

#### Scene 8 — game
Nathan adds decay to prediction memory.

Old information fades.

The guards stop behaving like psychics.

---

### 4 October — Sunday/world texture

#### Scene 9 — parallel households
Brief independent scenes:
- Claire grocery/household logistics;
- Phil preparing for work/client appointments;
- Jay dealing with business;
- Mitchell/Cameron managing Lily;
- Alex practicing cello;
- Haley dealing with her own social plans.

These can be short.

The purpose is not exposition.

The purpose is to make the world exist when Nathan is not there.

#### Scene 10 — Nathan
Weekly planning.

Nathan writes a realistic to-do list:
- school;
- football;
- prediction tuning;
- device testing.

He crosses out an overly ambitious item.

This is a rare instance where discipline wins over perfectionism.

---

## MOVEMENT II — 5–9 OCTOBER
### The game and the relationship both start to mature

### 5 October

#### Scene 11 — school
A classmate begins treating Nathan as a reliable person rather than a smart novelty.

#### Scene 12 — Alex
Alex walks with Nathan for part of the way after school.

No excuse.

Silence is not awkward enough to make either of them leave.

Nathan notices.

Then assumes she had nowhere else to go.

#### Scene 13 — game
Full pursuit test.

The player deliberately repeats the same escape route.

The prediction model learns.

One guard cuts the route.

Another checks a different probable exit.

Nathan smiles.

Now the system feels like the game he imagined.

---

### 6 October

#### Scene 14 — football
Competitive teammate and Nathan cooperate successfully.

The teammate earns more playing time.

Nathan is happy for him.

The teammate realizes Nathan is not trying to take his place.

Friendship develops.

#### Scene 15 — school
Teacher mentions a possible club/competition.

Nathan is interested.

He hesitates because joining something new means talking to people.

Alex later makes an offhand comment that it sounds like something he would be good at.

It matters more to him than it should.

#### Scene 16 — game
Nathan compares:
- A* baseline;
- flow field;
- full architecture.

The difference is measurable.

This can be one of the strongest technical scenes.

---

### Nathan thread

#### Scene 17 — ordinary school day
Nathan has his normal classes.

Alex returns from her day with her own opinions/problems.

Their lives are intersecting without merging.

#### Scene 18 — evening
Nathan integrates prediction and flow field:
- prediction picks destination;
- field moves agents;
- steering resolves local conflicts.

The architecture finally works end-to-end.

#### Scene 19 — emotional echo
Alex and Nathan have a short conversation the next day about something unrelated to school.

Nathan begins to realize he likes talking to her even when he has nothing to say.

## MOVEMENT III — 8–11 OCTOBER
### The new normal and the first real weekend

### 8 October

#### Scene 20 — school
Teacher gives Nathan an unexpectedly difficult question.

Nathan answers.

Another student looks impressed.

Nathan becomes uncomfortable.

Teacher changes the subject rather than dwelling on it.

#### Scene 21 — Alex
Alex notices this reaction.

She teases him lightly.

Nathan laughs.

This is one of the first moments of uncomplicated interaction.

#### Scene 22 — game
Prediction has too much historical memory.

Nathan tunes the weighting:
recent behavior matters more than old behavior.

This foreshadows his later understanding of people:
old scripts are not enough.

---

### 9 October

#### Scene 23 — football
Maxwell asks Nathan to help outside formal practice.

This is the first real invitation.

Nathan says yes without overthinking.

Small but important.

#### Scene 24 — family
Melissa buys/arranges something for the rental.

The house acquires another normal household detail.

No exposition needed.

#### Scene 25 — game
First complete playthrough.

The game works.

Nathan wins the test.

Then finds six things he dislikes.

---

### 10 October — Saturday

#### Scene 26 — football match
Nathan's team plays.

He performs well.

The coach publicly trusts him with a tactical adjustment.

Nathan does not become captain.

He is simply becoming important.

#### Scene 27 — post-match
Teammate invites him to do something afterward.

Nathan hesitates.

Then accepts.

This is probably his biggest social action of the episode.

#### Scene 28 — game
Late afternoon/evening.

Nathan tests the game with 20–24 enemies.

Performance is now acceptable.

The remaining problems are optimization and polish.

#### Scene 29 — Alex echo
Alex does not need to appear.

Nathan simply notices that he is looking forward to talking to her on Monday.

---

### 11 October — Sunday

#### Scene 30 — family / local life
A short family outing.

The rental neighborhood now feels familiar.

Someone mentions how long they will stay.

Nathan realizes they may actually buy here eventually.

Do not resolve the future house yet.

#### Scene 31 — Nathan
Weekly plan.

He sees how much work remains.

Instead of working until 2 a.m., he stops at a reasonable hour.

This shows discipline.

---

## MOVEMENT IV — 12–15 OCTOBER
### Final push

### 12 October

#### Scene 32 — school pressure
A normal assignment collides with game testing.

Nathan tries to do both perfectly.

He cannot.

#### Scene 33 — parent interaction
David or Melissa notices.

The parent does not tell him to abandon the game.

They tell him to choose what actually matters tonight.

Nathan learns prioritization.

#### Scene 34 — Alex
Alex notices he is tired.

She asks if he slept.

Nathan says yes.

She knows he is lying.

Not because she has magical insight.

Because she has now spent enough time around him.

This is a relationship milestone.

#### Scene 35 — game
Optimization:
- update frequencies;
- caching;
- compact state;
- fewer redundant calculations;
- device profiling.

The story should show the work through decisions rather than code.

---

### 13 October

#### Scene 36 — school
Teacher gives Nathan a small academic opportunity.

Nathan accepts.

He now initiates the "yes" himself.

#### Scene 37 — football
Teammate casually treats him as part of the group.

No ceremony.

That is the point.

#### Scene 38 — game
Old-device test.

The game runs.

One last performance cliff appears.

Nathan fixes it by reducing work, not by demanding more hardware.

#### Scene 39 — Alex
A short conversation.

Nathan makes a joke first.

Alex laughs.

He immediately thinks maybe she was just being polite.

The reader sees the mismatch.

---

## 14 October — The Incident

### Parallel canon thread

Dede arrives and the family fallout unfolds.

Show the family independently where that provides value.

visit or behavior. citeturn191690search60turn191690search3

### Nathan thread

#### Scene 40 — school
Nathan's school day continues.

He notices a change in Alex's mood only because he knows her a little now.

He asks.

She answers only partly.

He does not force it.

This is a good contrast with his future temptation to rely on canon knowledge.

#### Scene 41 — consequence
Later, Nathan encounters a natural consequence of the family event through an ordinary contact.

He does not solve anything.

He simply realizes that his memory predicted one reaction and reality produced another.

This should be an early butterfly-effect marker.

#### Scene 42 — game
Final heavy technical session.

The build reaches the target.

Nathan checks:
- 1 guard;
- 5;
- 10;
- 15;
- 20;
- 24.

It holds.

---

## 15 October — release

### Scene 43 — morning
Nathan is tired but functioning.

School feels normal.

That matters.

The reader should feel how far he has come in one month.

### Scene 44 — teacher
Teacher sees a strong piece of work.

Instead of praise alone, the teacher gives Nathan a harder follow-up.

Nathan likes that more than a compliment.

### Scene 45 — football
Maxwell asks him about the weekend.

No longer "the new kid."

Just Nathan.

### Scene 46 — Alex
Alex initiates another conversation.

Nathan stays without searching for an exit.

The conversation contains no academic excuse.

Their relationship is now clearly real even if neither calls it anything.

### Scene 47 — final build
Nathan packages the game.

The release process is deliberately small.

No instant app-store celebrity.

He sends a test/release build to a few people.

One technically inclined tester notices:
"The enemies move differently."

Nathan explains the architecture.

The tester becomes interested.

This is the first seed of later technical recognition.

### Scene 48 — final emotional beat
Nathan watches the finished game.

Several guards move through the level.

He likes it.

Then spots something he would still change.

He closes the profiler.

For tonight.

**End Episode 2.**

## Drafting warning

Never turn the schedule into a checklist.

A week can be represented by:
- one full scene;
- two short scenes;
- a paragraph bridge;
- or a time jump.

The underlying timeline is deliberately richer than the visible story.
