Status: DRAFT OUTLINE / expanded working draft

## Story window

**Main story: 1–15 October 2009; public-launch coda: 23 October 2009**

Drafting references: use [the dialogue naturalness standard](../../canon/narrative/DIALOGUE_NATURALNESS_STANDARD.md), [Season 1 relationship pacing](../../canon/narrative/SEASON_1_RELATIONSHIP_PACING.md), [Maxwell's working profile](../../canon/characters/MAXWELL_WHITAKER.md), and [the foreknowledge/intervention policy](../../canon/narrative/FOREKNOWLEDGE_INTERVENTION_POLICY.md). Target finished length: **10,000–14,000+ words**.

The episode should feel like the beginning of normal life after the move. The game is the strongest through-line,
but the episode must contain multiple independent plots.

## Central movement

**Nathan is no longer trying to become settled; he is settled enough to start building something.**

The game reaches completion around the middle/later part of the episode.

The emotional plot is quieter:
Nathan and Alex become comfortable enough that their conversations no longer require an excuse.

## MOVEMENT I — 1–6 OCTOBER
### Becoming part of the routine

### 1 October

#### Scene 1 — school continuity
Do not reintroduce Nathan.

He knows the building.

He knows where to sit.

CA-MATH-01, the mathematics teacher who has seen Nathan's work since his transfer, remembers the stronger answer he gave in September.

This immediately tells the reader that the previous episode happened.

#### Scene 2 — Alex
Alex starts a conversation without needing a book or assignment.

The conversation is short.

Nathan almost leaves, then stays.

This is a small victory.

#### Scene 3 — football
Maxwell asks Nathan for help on a tactical situation.

Nathan gives a useful answer.

Maxwell calls him by name casually.

The social bridge is now real.

#### Scene 4 — game
Prediction field produces a destination that is plausible but too precise.

A guard appears psychic.

Nathan hates it.

He needs uncertainty.

### 2–3 October

#### Scene 5 — school
CA-MATH-01 gives Nathan an optional harder exercise.

Nathan finishes it.

The same teacher asks what he does outside school.

Nathan mentions programming.

Nothing dramatic.

Teacher stores the information.

This is the seed for a future academic opportunity.

#### Scene 6 — Alex / a small observation
During an ordinary conversation, Alex makes a small positive comment about something Nathan has done. He immediately downplays it, then carries on talking.

Alex notices the reaction but doesn't ask him to explain it or treat it as a pattern. They have had several short conversations, not enough for her to understand the cause or call out a habit. This is another quiet data point, not a confrontation or a relationship milestone.

#### Scene 7 — family
Short Callahan-family domestic sequence:
- boxes are mostly gone;
- kitchen routine is established;
- Heather knows where things are;
- Clifford is working longer hours.

The temporary rental begins to have ordinary life.

#### Scene 8 — game
Nathan adds decay to prediction memory.

Old information fades.

The guards stop behaving like psychics.

No formal team practice is assumed on Friday. Maxwell asks Nathan after school to look over one tactical situation or help him prepare for the next session/match. It is the first direct invitation to spend time together outside the normal team setting.

Nathan hesitates briefly, then says yes. Record the exact time and place they agree on; this is a small but meaningful social step, not an instant best-friend declaration.

#### Scene 24 — family / study area
Heather arranges a narrow second-hand bookshelf for Nathan's study area. It is acquired locally and placed beside his desk; choose the actual source in prose and record a price only if it is stated. No exposition is needed. The shelf gives the household a practical sign of settling in.

#### Scene 25 — game
First complete playthrough.

The game works.

Nathan wins the test.

Then finds six things he dislikes.

### 10 October — Saturday

#### Scene 26 — Saturday football event
Nathan's new California team plays a friendly/internal scrimmage or coach-run assessment. Do not call it an official league fixture unless player registration, eligibility, opponent and competition are separately established.

Nathan performs well enough for Coach Lance to trust him with one tactical adjustment, but he is not made captain. The trust is evidence-based and limited to the moment. Record the selected players, roles/minutes, observed action, and any teammate/coach state change in the scene ledger.

#### Scene 27 — post-match invitation
Maxwell invites Nathan to a low-stakes activity after the scrimmage, such as getting a snack nearby or talking for a few minutes before they leave. Avoid creating an unplanned party or ignoring parent/transport constraints.

Nathan hesitates because he fears imposing, then accepts. The milestone is that he accepts the invitation, not that the whole team suddenly becomes close.

#### Scene 28 — game
Late afternoon/evening.

Nathan first tests the game with 20–24 enemies; performance is now acceptable, though the remaining problems are optimization and polish.

After the stress test, Maxwell and one teammate try a few rounds in the current Mac/simulator build during a short, parent-approved visit to the Callahan rental. Nathan watches without coaching them and records what is unclear as they play. Their feedback is a usability check, not evidence of real-device performance or a request to expand the game's scope. Any specific observation that affects later fixes must be recorded in the ledger.

#### Scene 29 — Alex echo

## MOVEMENT IV — 12–15 OCTOBER
### Final push

### 12 October

#### Scene 32 — Alex notices a little fatigue
**Time:** 12:20–12:30, during the school lunch break.
Alex notices that Nathan is a little slower to answer and seems less rested than usual. She asks whether he slept enough. Nathan says he did sleep; she notices a small sign of fatigue but does not know its cause, and the narration must not claim she can read a lie. The tiredness is mild and cumulative after a busy weekend, not the result of an all-night coding session. This is a brief check-in, not an intimate diagnosis.

#### Scene 33 — school pressure after classes
**Time:** 15:20–16:20, after the school day.
A normal assignment due Wednesday 14 October competes with game work. Nathan initially tries to do both perfectly, then works out what the assignment actually requires. He must choose which optional game-polish task to defer; do not let the assignment or its deadline vanish after this scene.

#### Scene 34 — Heather helps him prioritize
**Time:** 17:00–17:20, Callahan rental.
Heather sees the assignment and asks about the real deadline. She helps Nathan compare the school requirement with the optional polish task without ordering him to abandon the game. Nathan commits to finishing the assignment first and limiting tonight's programming to one measured task. Record the exact deferred game task and keep the choice in continuity.

#### Scene 35 — targeted game optimization
**Time:** 20:00–22:00, Nathan's computer.
After dinner and the school assignment, Nathan spends his normal two-hour programming block on one measured bottleneck, such as a redundant update or cache lookup. He tests the change against a recorded scenario. This is not the whole bug list being magically fixed in one evening; unresolved issues remain for the later test/regression pass.

### 13 October

#### Scene 36 — school / academic opportunity
**Time:** 10:20–10:30, outside CA-MATH-01's classroom after a lesson.
The teacher follows up on the optional maths-enrichment/competition group and gives Nathan the information and next steps. Nathan asks for details and says he intends to pursue it, but still needs to check the meeting time against football before committing to attendance. No enrollment is implied.

#### Scene 37 — Alex / Nathan initiates
**Time:** 12:20–12:30, school lunch break.
Nathan starts a short conversation with a small joke. Alex laughs naturally; Nathan briefly wonders whether she is only being polite, but that is his interpretation, not the narration's verdict. This is an incremental social step, not a sign that Alex has confessed or that he now reads her feelings accurately.

#### Scene 38 — Tuesday football practice
**Time:** 16:00–17:30, California team's scheduled training.
Maxwell includes Nathan in a drill and uses the team's shorthand without explaining every routine. Nathan is not captain; his role and minutes remain Coach Lance's decision. The interaction shows accumulated familiarity rather than a sudden promotion.

#### Scene 39 — old-device technical test
**Time:** 19:15–21:15, Callahan home.
Nathan tests the private build on his existing second-generation iPod touch. He records OS, build identifier, scenario and actual result. A performance cliff appears under the stress case; he reduces redundant work and verifies that the measured case now passes. Other gameplay issues stay open until separately tested. This is still private testing, not a public release.

## 14 October — The Incident

### Parallel canon thread — working clock schedule
These clock times are a **working AU schedule**, not times established by the original episode. Nathan's California school day runs approximately 08:00–14:45.

- **16:15–16:45:** DeDe makes the unexpected first visit to Mitchell and Cameron and asks Mitchell to help arrange an apology/reconciliation with the family.
- **16:50–17:15:** Mitchell passes the news to Jay privately. The adults decide to handle the wider family meeting later; Nathan is not present and learns none of these private details.
- **18:45–20:30:** the family dinner at Claire and Phil's house. DeDe attempts to make amends; the apology goes badly and the confrontation with Gloria follows. Keep the established canon causes and reactions. Alex is at the family home for the dinner in this AU continuity, but she is not made a driver of the conflict or an interpreter of the adults' motives.

### Nathan thread

#### Scene 40 — school, before DeDe's visit
**Time:** 12:20–12:35, school lunch break.
Nathan notices Alex is distracted and asks a low-pressure question. Her immediate concern is an ordinary school assignment she has been struggling to finish before the next day; she tells him only that she has a lot to do. **This scene happens before DeDe's arrival**, so Alex cannot be reacting to that encounter yet. Nathan sees a small, observable change, not a private family cause, and does not interrogate her.

#### Scene 41 — consequence through Phil, after the dinner
**Time:** approximately 20:40–20:50, shared front walk/driveway as the dinner winds down.
Nathan is returning a football or helping bring an ordinary item inside when he crosses paths with Phil outside the neighboring home. Phil has just come through the tense family evening and is less buoyant than usual. Their exchange remains brief and neighborly; Phil does not confide in Nathan, and Nathan cannot infer the argument's private details from one strained interaction. This is the first visible effect of the event on someone Nathan already knows, not a reason for him to intervene.

#### Scene 42 — final heavy technical session
**Time:** 21:00–23:00, Callahan home.
Nathan resumes testing after the neighborhood encounter. He checks 1, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 24 guards against the recorded scenarios. The target cases now pass after targeted fixes; if any release-blocking defect appears, he fixes it and reruns the affected case rather than leaving it for the public version. Record the build and results. The next day's peer test remains useful for playability and regression, not as an excuse to ship a known critical bug.

### 15 October — package and private test

#### Scene 43 — morning / ordinary school
**Time:** 08:05–08:20, before the first lesson.
Nathan is tired but functioning. School feels normal, showing how far he has settled in without implying that every problem has disappeared.

#### Scene 44 — teacher / harder follow-up
**Time:** 10:10–10:25, class.
CA-MATH-01 offers a harder follow-up instead of giving praise alone. Nathan responds well to having something specific to solve. Any later club opportunity still depends on the actual follow-up and signup, not this moment alone.

#### Scene 45 — Alex initiates, the day after the dinner
**Time:** Thursday 15 October, 12:20–12:30, school lunch break.
Alex starts a non-academic conversation. She is a little more preoccupied than usual after the previous evening's family dinner, but chooses an ordinary topic and does not owe Nathan an explanation. If she mentions the dinner, she shares only what she wants; otherwise Nathan cannot know its details. He stays rather than looking for an exit. Their comfort grows without a confession or label.

#### Scene 46 — football and Maxwell's weekend invitation
**Time:** 16:00–17:30, scheduled California training; invitation at 17:32–17:40 after the session.
After Thursday's training, Maxwell asks whether Nathan would like to get a snack with a couple of teammates after their usual Saturday team activity. It extends the earlier post-match invitation; it is not a new official fixture or a promise about playing time. Nathan is interested but needs to check parent permission and transport.

#### Scene 47 — Wesley's final private playtest and submission
**Time:** 18:30–20:00, Callahan home, with Clifford available for the device/account administration.
Wesley tests the candidate on Nathan's second-generation iPod touch. The test must have a concrete purpose: Wesley attempts a short level without Nathan coaching him, and Nathan records any point where the objective, controls or enemy feedback is unclear. Nathan then makes any release-blocking fix and reruns the affected test, followed by a final regression pass against the documented guard-count scenarios. No known critical defect is left in the version submitted. Clifford handles the adult account/submission steps; the candidate is submitted for App Store review by approximately 21:30. This is a small, controlled test, not an audience launch.

#### Scene 48 — final emotional beat
**Time:** 21:45–22:00, Nathan's room.
Nathan watches the final tested build. He is pleased that it works and still notices non-blocking improvements he could make later. He closes the profiler for tonight. The main action ends with the candidate submitted for review, not yet public.

## 23 October — public launch after review

#### Scene 49 — the game goes public
The App Store approves the submitted version under this working timeline, and the game becomes publicly available on 23 October, eight days after submission. The exact review duration is a working story date, not a claim that approval was guaranteed.

Nathan has already run his own technical tests and had peers try the game before submission. The public launch is therefore a payoff to those checks, not an impulsive upload of an untested prototype. Clifford helped with the adult account and administrative steps; Nathan remains the author of the game.

The first public availability does not make him famous. Downloads, reviews and wider recognition remain unknown until there is actual evidence. Nathan is pleased that people outside his small test circle can now get the game, while immediately noticing a possible future improvement.
