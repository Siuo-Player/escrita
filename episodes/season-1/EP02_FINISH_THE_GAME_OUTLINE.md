Status: DRAFT OUTLINE / expanded working draft

## Story window

**Main story: 1–15 October 2009; public-launch coda: 23 October 2009**

Drafting references: use [the dialogue naturalness standard](../../canon/narrative/DIALOGUE_NATURALNESS_STANDARD.md), [Season 1 relationship pacing](../../canon/narrative/SEASON_1_RELATIONSHIP_PACING.md), [Maxwell's working profile](../../canon/characters/MAXWELL_WHITAKER.md), and [the foreknowledge/intervention policy](../../canon/narrative/FOREKNOWLEDGE_INTERVENTION_POLICY.md). Scene beats are constraints, not dialogue to expand mechanically; keep *The Incident* parallel unless an established, credible interaction gives Nathan a reason and a way to help.

Target finished length: **10,000–14,000+ words**.

The episode should feel like the beginning of normal life after the move. The game is the strongest through-line,
but the episode must contain multiple independent plots.

## Central movement

**Nathan is no longer trying to become settled; he is settled enough to start building something.**

The game reaches completion around the middle/later part of the episode.

The emotional plot is quieter:
Nathan and Alex become comfortable enough that their conversations no longer require an excuse.

## MOVEMENT I — 1–6 OCTOBER
### Becoming part of the routine

### 1 October

#### Scene 1 — school continuity
Do not reintroduce Nathan.

He knows the building.

He knows where to sit.

CA-MATH-01, the mathematics teacher who has seen Nathan's work since his transfer, remembers the stronger answer he gave in September.

This immediately tells the reader that the previous episode happened.

#### Scene 2 — Alex
Alex starts a conversation without needing a book or assignment.

The conversation is short.

Nathan almost leaves, then stays.

This is a small victory.

#### Scene 3 — football
Maxwell asks Nathan for help on a tactical situation.

Nathan gives a useful answer.

Maxwell calls him by name casually.

The social bridge is now real.

#### Scene 4 — game
Prediction field produces a destination that is plausible but too precise.

A guard appears psychic.

Nathan hates it.

He needs uncertainty.

---

### 2–3 October

#### Scene 5 — school
CA-MATH-01 gives Nathan an optional harder exercise.

Nathan finishes it.

The same teacher asks what he does outside school.

Nathan mentions programming.

Nothing dramatic.

Teacher stores the information.

This is the seed for a future academic opportunity.

#### Scene 6 — Alex / observation
Alex notices Nathan reacts strangely when praised.

She asks why.

He gives an evasive answer.

Alex does not push.

This is important because she is beginning to observe him.

#### Scene 7 — family
Short Callahan-family domestic sequence:
- boxes are mostly gone;
- kitchen routine is established;
- Heather knows where things are;
- Clifford is working longer hours.

The temporary rental begins to have ordinary life.

#### Scene 8 — game
Nathan adds decay to prediction memory.

Old information fades.

The guards stop behaving like psychics.

---

### 4 October — Sunday/world texture

#### Scene 9 — parallel households
Brief independent scenes:
- Claire grocery/household logistics;
- Phil preparing for work/client appointments;
- Jay dealing with business;
- Mitchell/Cameron managing Lily;
- Alex practicing cello;
- Haley dealing with her own social plans.

These can be short.

The purpose is not exposition.

The purpose is to make the world exist when Nathan is not there.

#### Scene 10 — Nathan
Weekly planning.

Nathan writes a realistic to-do list:
- school;
- football;
- prediction tuning;
- device testing.

He crosses out an overly ambitious item.

This is a rare instance where discipline wins over perfectionism.

## MOVEMENT II — 5–9 OCTOBER
### The game and the relationship both start to mature

### 5 October

#### Scene 11 — school
Wesley Moss begins treating Nathan as a reliable person rather than a smart novelty.

#### Scene 12 — Alex
Alex walks with Nathan for part of the way after school.

No excuse.

Silence is not awkward enough to make either of them leave.

Nathan notices.

Then assumes she had nowhere else to go.

#### Scene 13 — game
Full pursuit test.

The player deliberately repeats the same escape route.

The prediction model learns.

One guard cuts the route.

Another checks a different probable exit.

Nathan smiles.

Now the system feels like the game he imagined.

---

### 6 October

#### Scene 14 — football
Maxwell and Nathan cooperate successfully on a concrete tactical situation.

Maxwell earns more playing time because he executes the work consistently in training; Nathan's advice is one contribution, not the sole reason for the coach's decision.

Nathan is happy for him. Maxwell realizes Nathan is not trying to take his place. Their friendship develops by one step.

#### Scene 15 — school
CA-MATH-01 mentions the school's optional maths-enrichment/competition group, following up on the earlier suggestion.

Nathan is interested.

He hesitates because joining something new means talking to people.

Alex later makes an offhand comment that it sounds like something he would be good at.

It matters more to him than it should.

#### Scene 16 — game
Nathan compares:
- A* baseline;
- flow field;
- full architecture.

The difference is measurable.

This can be one of the strongest technical scenes.

#### Scene 17 — ordinary school day
Nathan has his normal classes. During an ordinary encounter in a shared school space, he mentions that CA-MATH-01 suggested the school's optional maths-enrichment/competition group. Alex makes an offhand, encouraging comment that it sounds like something he would be good at. Alex still has her own opinions and problems. Their lives intersect without merging, and this conversation doesn't insert Nathan into the private events of the canon episode.

#### Scene 18 — evening
Nathan integrates prediction and flow field:
- prediction picks destination;
- field moves agents;
- steering resolves local conflicts.

The architecture finally works end-to-end.

#### Scene 19 — emotional echo (Thursday 8 October, morning)
Before the other school beats on 8 October, Alex and Nathan have a short conversation about something unrelated to school. It is brief and ordinary; neither needs a new excuse to speak.

Nathan begins to realize he likes talking to her even when he has nothing to say. This is his realization, not evidence that Alex has already identified her feelings.

## MOVEMENT III — 8–11 OCTOBER
### The new normal and the first real weekend

### 8 October

#### Scene 20 — school
CA-MATH-01 gives Nathan an unexpectedly difficult question.

Nathan answers.

Another student looks impressed.

Nathan becomes uncomfortable.

Teacher changes the subject rather than dwelling on it.

#### Scene 21 — Alex
Alex notices this reaction.

She teases him lightly.

Nathan laughs.

This is one of the first moments of uncomplicated interaction.

#### Scene 22 — game
Prediction has too much historical memory.

Nathan tunes the weighting:
recent behavior matters more than old behavior.

This foreshadows his later understanding of people:
old scripts are not enough.

---

### 9 October

#### Scene 23 — football invitation (Friday 9 October)
No formal team practice is assumed on Friday. Maxwell asks Nathan after school to look over one tactical situation or help him prepare for the next session/match. It is the first direct invitation to spend time together outside the normal team setting.

Nathan hesitates briefly, then says yes. Record the exact time and place they agree on; this is a small but meaningful social step, not an instant best-friend declaration.

#### Scene 24 — family / study area
Heather arranges a narrow second-hand bookshelf for Nathan's study area. It is acquired locally and placed beside his desk; choose the actual source in prose and record a price only if it is stated. The bowl from Seattle and the framed Washington-coast family photograph are already unpacked; this shelf is an additional item, not a replacement for the earlier props.

No exposition is needed. The shelf gives the household a practical sign of settling in.

#### Scene 25 — game
First complete playthrough.

The game works.

Nathan wins the test.

Then finds six things he dislikes.

---

### 10 October — Saturday

#### Scene 26 — Saturday football event
Nathan's new California team plays a friendly/internal scrimmage or coach-run assessment. Do not call it an official league fixture unless player registration, eligibility, opponent and competition are separately established.

Nathan performs well enough for Coach Lance to trust him with one tactical adjustment, but he is not made captain. The trust is evidence-based and limited to the moment. Record the selected players, roles/minutes, observed action, and any teammate/coach state change in the scene ledger.

#### Scene 27 — post-match invitation
Maxwell invites Nathan to a low-stakes activity after the scrimmage, such as getting a snack nearby or talking for a few minutes before they leave. Avoid creating an unplanned party or ignoring parent/transport constraints.

Nathan hesitates because he fears imposing, then accepts. The milestone is that he accepts the invitation, not that the whole team suddenly becomes close.

#### Scene 28 — game
Late afternoon/evening.

Nathan first tests the game with 20–24 enemies; performance is now acceptable, though the remaining problems are optimization and polish.

After the stress test, Maxwell and one teammate try a few rounds in the current Mac/simulator build during a short, parent-approved visit to the Callahan rental. Nathan watches without coaching them and records what is unclear as they play. Their feedback is a usability check, not evidence of real-device performance or a request to expand the game's scope. Any specific observation that affects later fixes must be recorded in the ledger.

#### Scene 29 — Alex echo
Alex does not need to appear.

Nathan simply notices that he is looking forward to talking to her on Monday.

---

### 11 October — Sunday

#### Scene 30 — family / local life
During a short family outing, the rental neighborhood feels more familiar.

Heather says that if Clifford's new role proves stable, she can imagine the family eventually buying a home in the area; she is expressing a hope, not announcing a decision. Nathan realizes that California may become a lasting home rather than a temporary stop.

Do not resolve the future house yet.

#### Scene 31 — Nathan
Weekly plan.

He sees how much work remains.

Instead of working until 2 a.m., he stops at a reasonable hour.

This shows discipline.

## MOVEMENT IV — 12–15 OCTOBER
### Final push

### 12 October

#### Scene 32 — school pressure
A normal assignment collides with game testing.

Nathan tries to do both perfectly.

He cannot.

#### Scene 33 — Heather notices
Heather notices Nathan trying to juggle the assignment and game work, asks what is due, and helps him compare the actual deadline with the optional polish task. She does not order him to abandon the game or give a generic motivational speech.

Nathan chooses one priority and explicitly defers the other. Record which assignment/task is deferred and its real deadline so it cannot vanish later.

#### Scene 34 — Alex
Alex notices he is tired.

She asks if he slept.

Nathan says yes.

She knows he is lying.

Not because she has magical insight.

Because she has now spent enough time around him.

This is a relationship milestone.

#### Scene 35 — game
Optimization:
- update frequencies;
- caching;
- compact state;
- fewer redundant calculations;
- device profiling.

The story should show the work through decisions rather than code.

---

### 13 October

#### Scene 36 — school / academic opportunity
CA-MATH-01 follows up on the optional maths-enrichment/competition group and gives Nathan the information and next steps.

Nathan asks for the details and says he intends to pursue it, but checks the meeting time against football before committing to attendance. This is the first time he initiates a concrete next step himself; it does not mean he is already enrolled.

#### Scene 37 — Tuesday football practice
At the scheduled California training session on 13 October, Maxwell includes Nathan in a drill and uses the team's shorthand with him without explaining every routine. Nathan is not captain; his role and minutes still depend on Coach Lance.

No ceremony. That is the point. Record who initiated the interaction and what the others observed.

#### Scene 38 — game
Old-device test.

The game runs.

One last performance cliff appears.

Nathan fixes it by reducing work, not by demanding more hardware.

#### Scene 39 — Alex
A short conversation.

Nathan makes a joke first.

Alex laughs.

He immediately thinks maybe she was just being polite.

The reader sees the mismatch.

### 15 October — package and private test

#### Scene 43 — morning
Nathan is tired but functioning.

School feels normal.

That matters.

The reader should feel how far he has come in one month.

#### Scene 44 — teacher
CA-MATH-01 sees a strong piece of work.

Instead of praise alone, the teacher gives Nathan a harder follow-up.

Nathan likes that more than a compliment.

#### Scene 45 — football
After Thursday's scheduled California training, Maxwell asks whether Nathan would like to get a snack with a couple of teammates after their usual Saturday team activity. It extends the earlier post-match invitation; it is a tentative social plan, not a new official fixture or a promise about playing time. Nathan says he is interested but will check with his parents about transport. He is no longer treated simply as "the new kid."

#### Scene 46 — Alex
Alex initiates another conversation.

Nathan stays without searching for an exit.

The conversation contains no academic excuse.

Their relationship is now clearly real even if neither calls it anything.

#### Scene 47 — final build
Nathan packages the game.

The packaging/private-test process is deliberately small.

No instant app-store celebrity.

He packages the candidate and lets Wesley Moss, his technology/library acquaintance, try it on Nathan's second-generation iPod touch in a supervised final private playtest. Clifford has already handled the parent-managed signing/provisioning route. Wesley joins Maxwell's earlier playability feedback as a second peer test; he notices that the enemies move less predictably than familiar game enemies.

Nathan explains one part of the architecture in accessible terms. Wesley's interest is modest and based only on what he actually saw. Nathan folds in any actionable feedback, completes a last technical/regression pass and, with Clifford handling the adult account/submission administration, submits the candidate to the App Store on 15 October. Submission is not the same as publication; the story's working target is public availability after review on 23 October. No instant fame is assumed.

#### Scene 48 — final emotional beat
Nathan watches the finished game.

Several guards move through the level.

He likes it.

Then spots something he would still change.

He closes the profiler.

For tonight.

The main action closes on 15 October with the candidate submitted for review; the game is not public yet.

## 23 October — public launch after review

#### Scene 49 — the game goes public
The App Store approves the submitted version under this working timeline, and the game becomes publicly available on 23 October, eight days after submission. The exact review duration is a working story date, not a claim that approval was guaranteed.

Nathan has already run his own technical tests and had peers try the game before submission. The public launch is therefore a payoff to those checks, not an impulsive upload of an untested prototype. Clifford helped with the adult account and administrative steps; Nathan remains the author of the game.

The first public availability does not make him famous. Downloads, reviews and wider recognition remain unknown until there is actual evidence. Nathan is pleased that people outside his small test circle can now get the game, while immediately noticing a possible future improvement.

**End Episode 2.**
