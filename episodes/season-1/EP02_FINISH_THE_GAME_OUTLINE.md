# Season 1, Episode 2 — Finish the Game

Status: DRAFT OUTLINE / working
Updated: 2026-10-08

## Story window

**1–15 October 2009**

Target finished length: **10,000–14,000 words**.

Purpose: finish Nathan's first mobile game while making his new life feel established rather than newly
introduced. The technical arc is the spine; relationship, school, football, family adaptation and canon AU
threads advance independently.

## Threads

### A — Nathan / game
Prediction field → integration → optimization → device testing → release.

### B — Nathan + Alex
From early friendship into recognizable personal interest, without jumping into dating.

### C — School
Nathan's competence gains a reputation of its own; one teacher begins to create an opportunity for him.

### D — Football
Nathan earns a genuine place in the new team and begins one real peer relationship.

### E — Pierce family
California stops feeling temporary.

### F — Modern Family AU
Come Fly with Me on 7 Oct and The Incident on 14 Oct, generally shown in parallel unless Nathan naturally
intersects their consequences.

### G — background world
Props, errands, hobbies, class projects, family goals and other people's progress continue off-camera.

## Dated outline

### 1–4 Oct — new normal

- Open without reintroducing the cast.
- School feels familiar enough that Nathan knows where to go.
- Teacher references something he did the previous week.
- Football teammate asks Nathan for help with a tactical problem.
- Alex initiates a conversation that has no necessary homework excuse.
- Nathan is surprised but stays in the conversation.
- Game: prediction map produces plausible destinations but too many false positives.

Background:
- family begins using the new house normally;
- one ordinary shopping trip establishes a recurring household object;
- a Dunphy-family member has an unrelated goal visible in a short parallel cut.

### 5–6 Oct — prediction becomes useful

- Nathan tests repeated player escape patterns.
- He discovers one rule produces convincing anticipation but another makes the guards seem psychic.
- He tunes decay and uncertainty.
- Alex sees Nathan frustrated by the game and jokes with him.
- Nathan relaxes enough to joke back.
- A small moment later shows he immediately questions whether he was too familiar.

### 7 Oct — Come Fly with Me

**Canon parallel**
- Jay and Phil's model-airplane story takes place independently.
- Claire/Manny's sibling realization unfolds in the family thread.
- Nathan is not dragged into either plot.

**Nathan**
- Nathan has a normal school/football/programming day.
- At some point he learns enough about the family's day to know something is happening, but physical distance
  remains.
- In the evening he watches his latest build and realizes the prediction layer can choose between several
  possible destinations instead of one hard guess.
- Alex and Nathan have a short conversation the next day about something unrelated.

### 8–9 Oct — relationship progression

- Alex chooses to sit or walk with Nathan without an academic pretext.
- Nathan interprets it as convenience.
- A friend/teammate notices the two get along and makes a mild comment.
- Nathan deflects.
- School: teacher offers Nathan an optional harder problem or club/competition reference.
- Nathan initially worries this is only encouragement because he is new.
- Football: he starts earning trust during tactical play.

### 10–11 Oct — first meaningful weekend

- Saturday match/team activity.
- Nathan performs well and gets practical praise.
- His competitive teammate has to cooperate with him.
- Nathan enjoys the social side more than he expected.
- Alex may appear only briefly through school/weekend geography; she does not need to attend the match.
- Game: first full end-to-end run with multiple guards.
- The game almost works perfectly; Nathan finds several small performance problems.

### 12–13 Oct — pressure and refinement

- Schoolwork, football and game progress collide.
- Nathan tries to squeeze too much into one evening.
- A parent tells him to stop for the night rather than letting perfectionism eat all his time.
- Nathan agrees, then thinks about the bug anyway.
- Alex notices he is distracted.
- Their conversation reveals that she knows him well enough to recognize the pattern.
- This is the relationship milestone: **Alex has started observing Nathan, not merely speaking to him.**

Technical:
- update-frequency reduction;
- caching;
- compact data structures;
- constrained-device testing;
- tuning for 2009 hardware.

### 14 Oct — The Incident

**Canon parallel**
- Dede arrives and the family fallout unfolds.
- Nathan is not inserted into the gathering unless a genuinely natural connection exists.
- The main canon scene should stand on its own.

**Consequence option**
- Nathan encounters one family member later, or hears about the aftermath through an ordinary interaction.
- He notices that a remembered reaction did not happen exactly as he expected.
- This is an early butterfly-effect marker.

**Nathan**
- final heavy optimization session.
- The game reaches the target frame rate and stable behavior.
- Nathan has no reason to celebrate yet; he immediately identifies cosmetic or balance issues.

### 15 Oct — release

- School morning: Nathan is tired but functional.
- A teacher notices he has done something unusually good on a class task.
- Football remains a normal part of his day.
- Alex and Nathan share an easy conversation that would have been impossible two weeks earlier.
- Final device playtest.
- Build is packaged.
- Nathan gives a copy to a friend/teammate or sends it to a small test group.
- A technically minded person can notice the enemy behavior and ask how it works; no fame is required.
- Nathan's reaction to praise is underwhelming because he sees every imperfection.

## Relationship endpoint

At the end of Episode 2:

Nathan and Alex are not a couple.

They are, however:

- comfortable enough to seek each other out sometimes;
- familiar enough to joke;
- aware of one another's habits;
- capable of talking without an excuse;
- each carrying a slightly different interpretation of the other.

Nathan still strongly underestimates Alex's interest.

## Episode state at the end

Nathan:
- settled into school;
- has the beginnings of a social circle;
- has earned a place in the new football environment;
- is increasingly comfortable around Alex;
- has finished and released the game.

Game:
- complete;
- playable;
- optimized;
- technically interesting;
- modest in content;
- not famous.

Alex:
- knows Nathan beyond his academic ability;
- has started initiating contact for social reasons;
- is curious about him;
- has begun noticing his insecurity without necessarily naming it.

Pierce family:
- California is becoming home rather than a temporary stop.

## Word-count guidance

At least 10,000 words.

The extra length should come from strong scenes and independent threads, not repetitive coding montages.

When the episode would otherwise be short, expand:

- Alex/Nathan scenes;
- the football relationship;
- teacher interactions;
- family adjustment;
- parallel canon material that has narrative value;
- consequences of the move;
- a background thread that can continue later.

Do not mechanically show every purchase, class, practice or bug.
