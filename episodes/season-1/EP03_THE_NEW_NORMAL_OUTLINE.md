# Season 1, Episode 3 — Working Outline: The New Normal

Status: DRAFT / BACKPROPAGATION-READY
Updated: 2026-10-08

## Story window

**Mid-October 2009**, beginning immediately after the game release and the 15 October state.

Exact end date is intentionally open until the surrounding Season 1 calendar is backpropagated.

## Central movement

**Nathan discovers that having a routine is different from belonging to one.**

Episode 1 was displacement.
Episode 2 established routine.
Episode 3 should make other people start expecting Nathan to be there.

## Main threads

### A — School identity
Nathan is no longer introduced as the new student.

A teacher asks him to help with a difficult academic task, but the task should have a cost:
time, attention or social exposure.

### B — Football friendship
The teammate who initially saw Nathan as competition now seeks him out voluntarily.

The friendship should begin through activity rather than a declaration.

### C — Nathan/Alex
Their conversations become ordinary.

Key progression:
- no book;
- no assignment;
- no authorial excuse.

One conversation should end with Nathan realizing only afterward that he stayed because he wanted to.

### D — Canon intervention
Nathan encounters a relatively small problem connected to the contemporary family.

He can help, but the episode should explicitly show the distinction:

**helping is not the same as controlling.**

His intervention produces one good immediate result and one small unintended consequence.

### E — Callahan family
Clifford's California job reaches its first stable rhythm.

Heather starts treating the temporary rental as a functioning home.

Nathan sees both parents adapting in ways he did not remember from his previous life because this family did not
exist in his remembered canon.

### F — Technical continuation
The first game is released, but the technical thread does not disappear.

A real person asks Nathan a serious technical question.

Nathan explains the architecture correctly, then realizes he cannot assume adults/peers will automatically
understand what seems obvious to him.

This creates the first bridge from hobbyist programming to communication/research.

## Suggested scene spine

1. **Morning after release** — Nathan sees the game is still just a small project.
2. **School** — teacher addresses him by name and asks for help.
3. **Alex** — ordinary conversation without academic pretext.
4. **Football** — teammate voluntarily includes Nathan.
5. **Family cutaway** — Claire/Phil or another family thread continues without Nathan.
6. **Canon intersection** — Nathan's small intervention.
7. **Consequence cutaway** — reader sees the unexpected second-order effect before Nathan does.
8. **Nathan reaction** — he initially interprets the result through remembered canon.
9. **Callahan home** — routine feels established.
10. **Technical conversation** — someone asks how the game handles so many agents.
11. **Alex echo** — she remembers or references something from an earlier interaction.
12. **Closing** — Nathan notices he has stopped thinking of every day in terms of "new school".

## Backpropagation targets

Episode 3 should create seeds for:

- first true academic mentor;
- first stable school friendship;
- later technical presentation;
- Nathan becoming more comfortable initiating short conversations;
- a future canon consequence that can be traced to the small intervention here.

## Constraint

Do not let Episode 3 become "Nathan is now popular."

His social circle is still small.

The milestone is that **other people now assume he belongs there too.**
