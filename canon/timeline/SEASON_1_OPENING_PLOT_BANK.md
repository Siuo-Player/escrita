# Season 1 Opening Plot Bank — September/October 2009

Status: WORKING / rich underlying timeline
Updated: 2026-10-08

This is a reservoir of events, goals and continuity seeds. It is deliberately richer than the final prose.
The drafting process selects from it.

## Nathan

### Technology
- 7 Sep: Nathan turns 13; the game project becomes the main personal project for the opening arc.
- 7–13 Sep: establishes the prototype, one map, enemy movement and conventional A* baseline.
- 14–15 Sep: move disrupts the development routine.
- 16–23 Sep: profiler exposes repeated pathfinding work; Nathan recognizes the flow-field technique he has
  already studied and adapts it.
- 24–30 Sep: flow field, steering and early prediction layer become integrated.
- 1–7 Oct: prediction/influence map becomes reliable.
- 8–12 Oct: optimization and constrained-device testing.
- 13–15 Oct: final playtest and release.
- Recurring character beat: whenever something works, Nathan immediately notices another imperfection.

### School
- Old school: teachers already know him as the unusually advanced but quiet computer student.
- Mathematics teacher: values his reasoning and sometimes makes him explain an answer more simply.
- Science teacher: recognizes his experimental thinking.
- English teacher: catches his perfectionist over-editing.
- New school after 16 Sep: teachers know him only through what they observe; no instant reputation.
- Possible recurring opportunity: a teacher later recommends an academic club/competition because of an
  earlier classroom performance.

### Football
- Before move: established captain; teammates know him and trust his decisions.
- After move: new team; he is not automatically captain.
- Early October: proves useful, earns trust, and begins one new peer relationship.
- One teammate may initially view him as competition for playing time.
- Nathan's social ease on the pitch contrasts with his hesitation in school corridors.

## Nathan + Alex

- 16 Sep: first sight at new school.
- 17 Sep: second sight/academic context; no approach.
- 18 Sep: Alex initiates practical first conversation.
- 21 Sep: ordinary school Monday; no birthday beat. Nathan turned 13 on 7 Sep, before the move. Any Alex interaction must build on the 18 Sep exchange rather than use a delayed birthday acknowledgment.
- 22 Sep: second interaction.
- 24–30 Sep: brief recurring conversations; Alex starts recognizing Nathan as a person rather than just
  the new smart kid.
- 1–7 Oct: one conversation continues after the original practical reason ends.
- 8–12 Oct: a shared joke, disagreement or small collaboration; Nathan misreads one signal.
- 13–15 Oct: Alex does something that quietly contradicts Nathan's pessimistic interpretation.
- End-state: they have a genuine early friendship/interest, not a relationship label.

## Callahan family

- 7 Sep: birthday/family routine before the move is announced.
- 8–13 Sep: promotion/relocation unfolds; Clifford's role had been quietly negotiated beforehand, but the confirmation is sudden to Nathan.
- 9 Sep: Seattle Public Schools and LAUSD both begin instruction; Nathan starts the school term at his old school. The school-calendar basis is documented in `research/narrative/OPENING_CALENDAR_AUDIT.md`.
- 10–13 Sep: Clifford receives/learns of promotion and California role; timing can be sudden from Nathan's
  perspective.
- 12–15 Sep: move preparation, packing, relocation travel and arrival in an employer-arranged temporary rental.
- 16–30 Sep: temporary-house setup, local errands, new routines and work demands; permanent home purchase remains a later subplot.
- 1–15 Oct: family starts acquiring favorite places, routines and objects even though the house itself is still temporary.

## Dunphy/Pritchett/Tucker background threads

These should not all appear in every episode.

- Claire keeps household logistics moving; ordinary errands can establish future props.
- Phil pursues hobbies and family activities independent of Nathan.
- Alex continues her own academic workload and family frustrations whether Nathan is present or not.
- Haley has her own social life and plans.
- Luke has independent childish projects/interests.
- Jay, Gloria and Manny retain their own family adjustments.
- Mitchell and Cameron continue building routines around Lily.
- A family member can acquire an object in an ordinary scene before it becomes important much later.

  later rather than participate directly.

## World-liveliness seeds

Potential tiny scenes to place only when useful:

- a parent buying school supplies;
- Claire returning from a supermarket with something later visible in the house;
- a package arriving during the move;
- Phil trying to assemble something;
- Alex studying while Nathan is programming elsewhere;
- a football teammate asking about a position;
- a teacher preparing a harder assignment for Nathan;
- Nathan noticing a new local store or route to school;
- a family member mentioning a purchase before the object becomes part of a joke;
- a recurring household item changing location because someone actually used it.

These are not checklist requirements. Their purpose is to make later continuity possible without retroactive
materialization.
