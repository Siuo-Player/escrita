**Parallel-world terminology:** episode titles below are labels from the television series Nathan watched
in his previous life. They identify remembered patterns and initial date anchors, not broadcasts or filmed

## A. Resolved foundation

1. **Protagonist:** Nathan Callahan (given name and family surname locked by the author on 2026-10-12), male, 13 at the story opening (having turned 13 that day); American;
   affluent technology-oriented family; high-performing student, especially in mathematics and computing; football/soccer captain; long-term physical
   training in strength, speed, flexibility, football and martial arts; shy, embarrassed by initiating
   interactions, perfectionist and emotionally sensitive; afraid of seeming arrogant; strongly afraid of
   rejection; strongly self-undervaluing; and reluctant to seek new friendships.
2. **Entry:** direct reincarnation into a new life at age 12, roughly two weeks before his thirteenth birthday,
   with previous-life memories.
3. **Starting point and calendar:** Season 1 begins on **Monday, 7 September 2009**, which is also Nathan's
   thirteenth birthday. LAUSD's 2009–10 school year begins on **Wednesday, 9 September 2009**. Nathan
   transfers one week later on **Wednesday, 16 September 2009**. In Nathan's previous life, the *Modern Family* Pilot was broadcast on
   **Wednesday, 23 September 2009**. That date is only the initial temporal anchor for corresponding lived
   family events in the parallel world; the broadcast itself did not happen here, and later divergence may
   move or replace those events. Nathan's first meaningful school connection is Alex; the first conversation is on 18 September. Other
   family members enter his life later through the encounters registered in the season chronology. There is
   no fixed number of family contacts required before the Pilot-memory date. He is one school year above Alex.
4. **Modern Family memory:** human, selective and fallible recollection of a programme he watched repeatedly.
   Major outcomes and memorable scenes may remain vivid, but details, dates, order and wording can be
   forgotten or misremembered. He has no eidetic memory, no access to off-screen events and no reliable
   ability to predict how real people will act. Familiarity gives him initial expectations, not certainty.
5. **Other previous-life knowledge:** memories of future-world facts beyond the television series are
   fragmented, uneven and secondary. They can suggest broad directions, not provide a perfect encyclopedia,
   exact future dates or guaranteed predictions.
6. **First family connection:** Nathan recognizes Alex at school and wants to speak to her but initially
   avoids it because of shyness and attraction. Alex initiates the first interaction after noticing that
   he has the right book/resource for an advanced class. Because Nathan arrives in the neighbourhood before the remembered Pilot date anchor, he can begin meeting
   family members through ordinary local life. The actual first-contact sequence is recorded in the Event
   Registry and Season 1 arc plan. There is no numerical contact target; Nathan still does not enter central
   family events unless the story gives him a natural reason.
7. **Independent life engine:** technology/computing, pursued through early programming, study, projects
   and eventual AI research.
8. **Long-term academic objective:** become an AI researcher and ultimately complete a credible AI-focused
   doctorate or equivalent research path. Dedicated AI doctoral programmes already existed by 2009, so
   the field itself does not need to be invented.
9. **Narrative proportion:** the remembered source-series plot normally occupies about 10–40% of an
   episode's plot focus, while characters from the show may occupy much more of the episode. Original
   material can center on Nathan/Alex, Nathan/Haley, school, football, technology, friends, parents or
   other themes.
10. **Season structure:** each story season represents roughly one year of Nathan's life. Season 1 begins
    on **7 September 2009**, while Nathan is still in his old city. Episode 1 covers **7–30 September**,
    compressing the old school, the father's sudden promotion, the family move, Nathan's first week in the
    new school, first interactions with Alex, the family events Nathan remembers under the *Pilot* label in parallel, and the following week. Episode 2
    covers **1–15 October** and completes the first mobile game around the middle or later part of the
    episode. The long story runs through adolescence, university and early professional life, ending in
    Nathan's early-to-mid twenties.
11. **Ending:** after Nathan and Alex are adults and established in education/work, the final scene is
    Alex telling Nathan that she is pregnant.
12. **Backpropagation:** later events must leave earlier traces. Pregnancy in particular needs a believable
    chain of prior adult relationship milestones, conception timing, subtle symptoms and earlier details
    that become meaningful in hindsight.
13. **Parallel family events:** Nathan does not need to participate in every lived family event merely because
    it corresponds to something he remembers from the source series. Those events can unfold in parallel and be referenced through thoughts,
    conversations or later consequences; those references should themselves create divergence rather than
    become episode recaps.
14. **Narration:** primarily Nathan's viewpoint, with occasional fluid access to nearby characters'
    reactions or thoughts when justified. The narration is epistemically bounded: what is presented must be
    compatible with what the characters could know, and character interpretations are not automatically
    objective truth.
15. **Butterfly effect:** strong and early. Nathan changes the timeline simply by existing and through
    altered information flow, meetings, timing and choices. Changes must accumulate rather than reset.
16. **Intervention:** Nathan actively tries to prevent tragedies and severe mistakes, especially early.
    He also tries to help with family and romantic conflicts, usually in a way compatible with his shy
    personality. He otherwise avoids aggressively rewriting every event. Some failures and unintended
    consequences are inevitable.
17. **Future-knowledge character arc:** Nathan initially treats the family partly as familiar behavioral
    patterns because he knows the series so well. As reality diverges, his predictions increasingly fail.
    He must relearn how to interact with them as individuals instead of as predictable characters.
18. **Technology realism:** Nathan does not invent unexplained future physics. His advantage comes from early
    study, recognizing promising directions, deriving or combining ideas and sustained practice. His
    software, games and AI work can be unusually advanced for the period when the result follows from what
    he has plausibly learned.

19. **Season 1 continuity spine:** a reusable school/football/programming rhythm is fixed for the first
    season. Nathan is in 8th grade and Alex in 7th, allowing natural school interaction while preserving
    their one-grade separation. At the end of the 2009–10 year, Nathan completes 8th grade and enters High School; Alex completes 7th grade and enters 8th grade. They do not graduate from middle school together. The default rhythm is programming on Monday/Wednesday/Friday,
    football training Tuesday/Thursday, a Saturday match, and lighter planning/programming on Sunday.
    Classroom scenes should reveal intelligence and personality through teacher interactions rather than
    repeated praise. The opening period includes Nathan's birthday on 7 September, his distant observations of Alex after the
    16 September school transfer, a practical first interaction initiated by Alex on 18 September, and a
    programming scene parallel to the remembered family events on 23 September. Full reusable schedule: `canon/timeline/SEASON_1_CONTINUITY.md`.

20. **Parallel-plot rule:** every episode should contain several simultaneously existing threads rather than
    a single plot plus filler. The normal mix is Nathan's immediate POV/life, family life and/or a thread based on events Nathan remembers from the *Modern Family* series in his previous life, relationship development, school/football or another recurring life thread, and
    one or more background threads carried by other characters. Threads may intersect, but they do not have
    to. Some should only appear briefly because the world continues when Nathan is not present.
21. **Persistent world / prop continuity:** recurring objects, household purchases, school materials, sports
    equipment and other visible details should have a believable origin. A later-used object can first appear
    being bought, delivered, unpacked, gifted or already owned and mentioned earlier. This applies to ordinary
    household details as well as recognizable props such as Nerf blasters. The goal is not to explain every
    object, but to avoid materializing important recurring things exactly when a scene needs them.
22. **Episode 2 subplot set:** the game remains the primary technical thread, but Episode 2 also carries a
    meaningful Nathan/Alex relationship progression, Nathan settling into the new school's academic/social
    environment, football/team integration, and the Callahan family's adjustment to California. The family events Nathan remembers under the source-series labels **Come Fly with Me** (7 October) and
    **The Incident** (14 October) provide parallel material; they should intersect Nathan only when his
    existing life naturally touches them. The story does not need an invented replacement family-event sequence just to fill the slot.

Detailed protagonist record: canon/characters/NATHAN_CALLAHAN.md.

## B. Still to decide — useful for the first arc

### 2. Nathan's personality weaknesses
**Resolved:** the selected weaknesses are fear of seeming arrogant, strong fear of rejection, strong
self-undervaluation (including appearance, intelligence, competence and romantic desirability), and
reluctance to seek new friendships. The broader self-undervaluation is a recurring engine of the
Nathan/Alex slow burn.

### 3. Parents' final occupations and family history
**Sufficiently bounded for the opening episodes.** Clifford (randomly selected given name; SSA 1960s male rank 159) remains a senior technology executive/early co-founder; Heather (randomly selected given name; SSA 1960s female rank 127) has a demanding, well-paid professional career outside core software engineering. Their roles, relationship with Nathan, and reason for the move are recorded in `canon/characters/CALLAHAN_FAMILY.md`. Keep exact employer/title and work subplots unspecified until the story needs them; both parents are supportive background, not a second main plot.

### 4. Nathan's first concrete technology project
**Resolved in design:** a small 2D mobile game for conservative 2009 hardware. Nathan builds the important
game logic himself in C++, but may use period-appropriate public/commercial frameworks and libraries and
purchased/commissioned art. The game's defining feature is advanced enemy movement and prediction:
a reusable grid-based flow field handles shared navigation efficiently, while a separate lightweight
player-behaviour memory creates a prediction/influence field that changes where enemies search or
intercept. The first implementation begins conventionally and becomes progressively more unusual as
Nathan optimizes it.

### 5. University destination
Eventually choose a plausible university/research environment and whether Nathan follows computer science,
AI, machine learning or another adjacent route before specializing.

### 6. First 2–4 recurring non-canon relationships
**First working peer defined:** Maxwell Whitaker, a competitive but fair teammate who wants more playing time. His name was selected with the reproducible random naming process and his profile is `canon/characters/MAXWELL_WHITAKER.md`. He gives Nathan a social foothold independent of Alex and the Dunphys. Add other recurring non-canon relationships when their independent goals create real story value; do not fill a quota with decorative characters.

### 7. Alex relationship pacing
**Season 1 direction established; later milestones remain open.** Nathan is attracted first because he recognizes Alex. Their opening exchanges are practical and Alex's initial interest is ordinary curiosity, not instant romance. The season should develop a believable friendship with gradual trust, no confession or official relationship as a required payoff. See `canon/narrative/SEASON_1_RELATIONSHIP_PACING.md`. Dating, mutual attraction becoming explicit, and later pauses remain undecided until the appropriate arc.

### 8. Haley complication
A later adult-only physical relationship between Nathan and Haley is permitted during a real separation
from Alex. The timing, cause and consequences should be decided only when the relevant adult arc is
planned. It must not function as consequence-free wish fulfillment.

### 9. Canon-character priority
**Working order of narrative focus:** Alex first, then the immediate Dunphy household (Claire, Phil, Haley,
Luke), followed by the Pritchett/Tucker side as credible shared situations arise. This is an attention
priority for the opening arc, not a numerical contact target, a fixed order for every person or a page-time
quota. The Event Registry controls dated first contacts. The remembered Pilot date can remain a parallel
family event without turning Nathan into a participant in the central family-reunion sequence.

### 10. Opening-week canon intersection

**Reviewed and resolved on 21 November 2026 (DEC-018):** the former target of having at least four family
members know Nathan by 23 September is retired. It was not an author-locked premise and conflicts with the
more natural contact sequence already established in the Event Registry: Alex's first conversation is
18 September, Phil's first meeting is 28 September, Claire's is 10 October, and Luke's is 22 October.
Do not move or invent introductions to satisfy a quota. Let each relationship begin when a credible
opportunity exists.

### 11. Canon changes and intervention priorities
**Policy established; specific first-year opportunities require a dated canon audit.** Use `canon/narrative/FOREKNOWLEDGE_INTERVENTION_POLICY.md` to separate certainty about remembered events from certainty about the altered timeline, rank the potential harm, assess Nathan's access and credibility, and choose the smallest plausible action. Nathan actively tries to prevent serious harm, but Episode 1–2 must not gain a manufactured crisis or place him in canon scenes without a credible reason. Once his action changes a causal condition, follow the consequence honestly. The remaining task is to map first-year canon events and identify only the opportunities that pass the feasibility test.

### 12. Episode length
**Resolved working target:** 10,000–14,000+ words for a typical opening episode. The story should reach that length through meaningful scenes, parallel threads and consequential detail, never filler.

### 13. Exact tone balance
Current target: comedy + warmth/family + slice-of-life + growth, with drama/romance appearing naturally.
