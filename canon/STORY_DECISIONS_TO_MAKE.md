## A. Resolved foundation

1. **Protagonist:** Nathan Callahan, male, 13 at the story opening (having turned 13 that day); American;
   affluent technology-oriented family; A-level student; football/soccer captain; long-term physical
   training in strength, speed, flexibility, football and martial arts; shy, embarrassed by initiating
   interactions, perfectionist and emotionally sensitive; afraid of seeming arrogant; strongly afraid of
   rejection; strongly self-undervaluing; and reluctant to seek new friendships.
2. **Entry:** direct reincarnation into a new life at age 12, only days before his thirteenth birthday,
   with previous-life memories.
3. **Starting point and calendar:** Season 1 begins on **Monday, 7 September 2009**, which is also Nathan's
   thirteenth birthday. LAUSD's 2009–10 school year begins on **Wednesday, 9 September 2009**. Nathan
   transfers one week later on **Wednesday, 16 September 2009**. The *Modern Family* pilot aired on
   **Wednesday, 23 September 2009**, and its in-universe events are kept on that date unless continuity
   requires otherwise. Nathan has already
   met at least four members of the family before/during the pilot through natural neighborhood,
   school and activity encounters, but should not be inserted into the family's main reunion scene
   unless the episode's existing action genuinely creates a place for him. He is one school year above
   Alex.
4. **Modern Family memory:** eidetic memory of everything actually shown in the series, including tiny
   details and dialogue, but memories must be actively retrieved. He has rewatched the series many times,
   so he has unusually deep familiarity with character behavior and can initially predict reactions with
   considerable accuracy.
5. **Future knowledge:** additional future-world memories are fragmented, non-personal and secondary.
   They are useful as directional hints rather than perfect prediction.
6. **First family connection:** Nathan recognizes Alex at school and wants to speak to her but initially
   avoids it because of shyness and attraction. Alex initiates the first interaction after noticing that
   he has the right book/resource for an advanced class. Because Nathan arrives in the neighborhood before
   the pilot, he can also meet several family members through ordinary local life. The working goal is
   that at least four family members already know him by the pilot, while Nathan still does not enter the
   family's main reunion scene unless the episode gives him a natural reason.
7. **Independent life engine:** technology/computing, pursued through early programming, study, projects
   and eventual AI research.
8. **Long-term academic objective:** become an AI researcher and ultimately complete a credible AI-focused
   doctorate or equivalent research path. Dedicated AI doctoral programmes already existed by 2009, so
   the field itself does not need to be invented.
9. **Narrative proportion:** the original *Modern Family* plot normally occupies about 10–40% of an
   episode's plot focus, while characters from the show may occupy much more of the episode. Original
   material can center on Nathan/Alex, Nathan/Haley, school, football, technology, friends, parents or
   other themes.
10. **Season structure:** each story season represents roughly one year of Nathan's life. Season 1 begins
    on **7 September 2009**, while Nathan is still in his old city. Episode 1 covers **7–30 September**,
    compressing the old school, the father's sudden promotion, the family move, Nathan's first week in the
    new school, first interactions with Alex, the Pilot in parallel, and the following week. Episode 2
    covers **1–15 October** and completes the first mobile game around the middle or later part of the
    episode. The long story runs through adolescence, university and early professional life, ending in
    Nathan's early-to-mid twenties.
11. **Ending:** after Nathan and Alex are adults and established in education/work, the final scene is
    Alex telling Nathan that she is pregnant.
12. **Backpropagation:** later events must leave earlier traces. Pregnancy in particular needs a believable
    chain of prior adult relationship milestones, conception timing, subtle symptoms and earlier details
    that become meaningful in hindsight.
13. **Canon parallelism:** Nathan does not need to participate in canon episodes merely because they are
    happening at the same time. Canon can unfold in parallel and be referenced through thoughts,
    conversations or later consequences; those references should themselves create divergence rather than
    become episode recaps.
14. **Narration:** primarily Nathan's viewpoint, with occasional fluid access to nearby characters'
    reactions or thoughts when justified. The narration is epistemically bounded: what is presented must be
    compatible with what the characters could know, and character interpretations are not automatically
    objective truth.
15. **Butterfly effect:** strong and early. Nathan changes the timeline simply by existing and through
    altered information flow, meetings, timing and choices. Changes must accumulate rather than reset.
16. **Intervention:** Nathan actively tries to prevent tragedies and severe mistakes, especially early.
    He also tries to help with family and romantic conflicts, usually in a way compatible with his shy
    personality. He otherwise avoids aggressively rewriting every event. Some failures and unintended
    consequences are inevitable.
17. **Future-knowledge character arc:** Nathan initially treats the family partly as familiar behavioral
    patterns because he knows the series so well. As reality diverges, his predictions increasingly fail.
    He must relearn how to interact with them as individuals instead of as predictable characters.
18. **Technology realism:** Nathan does not invent unexplained future physics. His advantage comes from early
    study, recognizing promising directions, deriving or combining ideas and sustained practice. His
    software, games and AI work can be unusually advanced for the period when the result follows from what
    he has plausibly learned.

19. **Season 1 continuity spine:** a reusable school/football/programming rhythm is fixed for the first
    season. Nathan is in 8th grade and Alex in 7th, allowing natural school interaction while preserving
    Alex's later middle-school graduation. The default rhythm is programming on Monday/Wednesday/Friday,
    football training Tuesday/Thursday, a Saturday match, and lighter planning/programming on Sunday.
    Classroom scenes should reveal intelligence and personality through teacher interactions rather than
    repeated praise. The opening period includes Nathan's birthday on 7 September, the 16 September
    school transfer, multiple small pre-Pilot contacts with Alex/Claire/Phil/Haley, and a
    pilot-parallel programming scene on 23 September. Full reusable schedule: `canon/timeline/SEASON_1_CONTINUITY.md`.

20. **Parallel-plot rule:** every episode should contain several simultaneously existing threads rather than
    a single plot plus filler. The normal mix is Nathan's immediate POV/life, family life and/or a *Modern
    Family* AU canon thread, relationship development, school/football or another recurring life thread, and
    one or more background threads carried by other characters. Threads may intersect, but they do not have
    to. Some should only appear briefly because the world continues when Nathan is not present.
21. **Persistent world / prop continuity:** recurring objects, household purchases, school materials, sports
    equipment and other visible details should have a believable origin. A later-used object can first appear
    being bought, delivered, unpacked, gifted or already owned and mentioned earlier. This applies to ordinary
    household details as well as recognizable props such as Nerf blasters. The goal is not to explain every
    object, but to avoid materializing important recurring things exactly when a scene needs them.
22. **Episode 2 subplot set:** the game remains the primary technical thread, but Episode 2 also carries a
    meaningful Nathan/Alex relationship progression, Nathan settling into the new school's academic/social
    environment, football/team integration, and the Callahan family's adjustment to California. On the
    *Modern Family* side, **Come Fly with Me** (7 October) and **The Incident** (14 October) provide canon
    AU material; they should intersect Nathan only when his existing life naturally touches them. The episode
    does not need an invented replacement canon episode.

## D. Narrative method decisions — established

The following are now part of the working methodology rather than open choices:

1. **Character lives first:** create detailed character schedules, private goals, actions and likely overlaps
   before selecting visible scenes.
2. **Scene assembly:** the final prose shows only a fraction of the underlying world. The writer's final job
   is to edit the richest internal reality into the strongest narrative sequence.
3. **Natural intersections:** approximately 90% of recurring interactions should arise from routines, prior
   decisions, geography, obligations or believable chains of cause and effect. Coincidences remain part of
   life, but should not be overused.
4. **Causal foreshadowing:** ordinary details may be deliberately shown because they later matter. This includes
   purchases, meals, routes, schedules and objects with believable provenance.
5. **Strong backpropagation:** when a later scene needs stronger causal support, revise backward through prior
   scenes and, if necessary, unseen history. Add an explanatory scene only when it improves the reader's
   understanding.
6. **Reader-visible butterfly chains:** small decisions may be shown from other characters' perspectives so
   the reader can see consequences accumulating before Nathan does.
7. **Dialogue first pass:** prioritize natural conversation; revise later for repetition, technical exposition
   and unnecessary explanation.
8. **Multiple cameras:** Nathan is the protagonist, not the permanent camera. Ensemble, character-centered and
   canon-parallel scenes are valid when they have independent narrative value.
9. **Canon independence:** canonical plots may be shown substantially without Nathan and should not be forced
   into his storyline.
10. **Revision order:** causal correctness → character motivation → relationship progression →
    timeline/provenance → scene order → pacing → repetition/exposition → sentence-level style.
