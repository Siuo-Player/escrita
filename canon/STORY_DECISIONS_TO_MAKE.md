# Story decisions to make

Status: WORKING
Purpose: decisions needed before building the first durable episode/arc.
Updated: 2026-10-07

The list is ordered by dependency. We do not need to decide every detail immediately. The first
group unlocks the actual story; later groups can remain provisional.

## A. Critical — needed first

### 1. Protagonist identity
- Name.
- Age at entry.
- Sex/gender.
- Nationality/background.
- Where he lives when the story begins.
- Family situation.
- Appearance only to the extent that it matters.
- Personality: strengths, flaws, habits, contradictions.
- What makes him interesting when no Modern Family character is present.

### 2. Entry mechanism
Choose one:
- reincarnation;
- transmigration;
- time travel;
- already existing original character who knows the future;
- another mechanism.

Also decide what happened immediately before he entered this life/world.

### 3. Knowledge of Modern Family
- Does he remember the whole series or only what he personally watched?
- How reliable is his memory?
- Does he know episode titles/details or mainly major events?
- Does he know events that happened off-screen?
- Does he know the finale/future beyond his starting date?
- Is his knowledge emotionally detached, nostalgic, or mixed?

Recommended default for this project:
He knows the series extremely well from his previous life, but memory is selective and gets less
reliable as the real timeline diverges.

### 4. Starting date and age
Choose the exact chronological entry point if possible.
This determines which canon characters are available and how much freedom we have.

### 5. Initial relationship to the family
How does he first enter the Modern Family orbit?
Examples:
- neighbour;
- school;
- family friend;
- new employee;
- relative of someone;
- sports/activity;
- chance meeting.

Also decide who is the first recurring connection.

### 6. Independent life engine
Choose the main thing the protagonist wants for himself before romance/major canon interference.
Examples:
- education;
- career;
- sport;
- music/art;
- technology;
- business;
- writing/film;
- travel;
- helping his own family;
- simply building a stable life.

This should create stories that would exist even without the sitcom.

## B. Important — defines the long-term shape

### 7. Main long-term ambition
What does "success" mean to him?
What would he pursue if Modern Family did not exist?

### 8. Main internal problem
What personal weakness or conflict makes life difficult?
Examples:
- fear of wasting his second chance;
- emotional isolation;
- perfectionism;
- guilt about changing other people's lives;
- difficulty trusting people;
- need for control;
- indecision.

### 9. Romance
Decide whether romance is:
- central;
- important but secondary;
- slow-burn;
- mostly absent at first.

If there is a likely partner, decide whether that is fixed early or allowed to emerge naturally.

Do not decide only by pairing preference. The relationship should arise from character compatibility,
timing, choices and consequences.

### 10. Canon divergence philosophy
How much should he try to change the future?
Possible modes:
- mostly observe;
- prevent major tragedies;
- selectively interfere;
- aggressively reshape events;
- initially observe, then gradually become willing to interfere.

Recommended default:
Selective interference. He helps when he has a credible reason and accepts that helping one person can
create new problems elsewhere.

### 11. How visible the future knowledge is
Should the protagonist ever say something like "I know this will happen"?
Or should the knowledge normally appear through subtle preparation, warnings, choices and intuition?

Recommended default:
Mostly subtle. Direct revelations should be rare and costly.

### 12. Starting social circle outside Modern Family
Pick 2–4 recurring non-canon people:
- friend;
- classmate;
- colleague;
- neighbour;
- mentor;
- rival;
- family member.

These characters stop the protagonist from existing only inside the sitcom.

## C. Story format

### 13. Episode length
Choose a normal target range:
- short: 2–4k words;
- medium: 4–8k;
- long: 8–15k+.

### 14. Episode cadence
Does every episode correspond roughly to a period/event from canon, or do we allow original episodes
between canon intersections?

Recommended default:
Original episodes can sit between canon-adjacent episodes. Canon chronology is the skeleton, not
the chapter list.

### 15. POV
Default close third person or first person?
Can we occasionally use another character's POV?

Recommended default:
Close third-person protagonist, with rare secondary POV when it reveals consequences he cannot see.

### 16. Tone
Set the normal mixture:
- comedy;
- warmth/family;
- drama;
- romance;
- mystery;
- introspection;
- slice of life;
- darker material.

The tone can vary by episode, but we need a recognizable baseline.

### 17. How much canon is shown directly
Choose:
- high canon density;
- medium;
- low.

Recommended default:
Medium. Recognizable canon relationships remain central, but original scenes carry substantial weight.

## D. World and realism

### 18. Geography
Where does the protagonist live, study and work?
How close is he to the Pritchett/Dunphy/Tucker families?

### 19. Money and practical constraints
How financially secure is he?
Does he need to work?
What can he realistically afford?

This matters because an overly capable protagonist with unlimited money quickly stops feeling grounded.

### 20. Competence
What is he genuinely good at?
What is he average/bad at?
What can he learn, and how long does it take?

Avoid a protagonist who is simultaneously a genius, athlete, entrepreneur, social mastermind and perfect
romantic lead.

### 21. Technology and future knowledge
Can he use knowledge of future technology/business/culture?
If yes:
- how much;
- how early;
- with what resources;
- with what legal/ethical constraints?

### 22. Ethical limits
What will he refuse to do even when future knowledge could make it profitable or convenient?
This becomes especially important once his interventions have consequences.

## E. The Modern Family side

### 23. Which canon characters matter most
Rank the initial recurring cast rather than trying to give everyone equal space.

### 24. Canon starting state
Are we preserving the exact canon chronology where possible, or intentionally changing a few dates from
the beginning?

### 25. Canon events to protect
Are there any major canon relationships/events you absolutely do not want changed?

### 26. Canon events we are allowed to change
Are there events you specifically want the protagonist to influence?

### 27. Character integrity
Are there any canon characters for whom you want especially strict characterization?

## F. Meta and publishing

### 28. Original ending
Should the story aim for:
- a planned ending;
- several major arcs with eventual closure;
- open-ended episodic life.

Recommended default:
Major arcs with a real ending, while allowing the world to continue between arcs.

### 29. Scale
How far do we expect the story to go?
Examples:
- one school year;
- several years;
- childhood → adulthood;
- through the series finale;
- beyond the series finale.

### 30. Crossovers
Should other fictional universes ever appear?
Choose:
- none;
- only tiny references;
- occasional crossover;
- planned crossover arcs.

Recommended default:
References are fine; full crossovers only when the protagonist's life makes the connection feel
natural.

## Minimum decisions to unblock Episode 1

We can start the actual story once these are fixed:

1. protagonist identity;
2. entry mechanism;
3. age + exact starting date;
4. what he remembers about Modern Family;
5. first connection to the family;
6. independent life engine;
7. main initial goal;
8. main internal problem;
9. tone;
10. initial romance policy.

Everything else can remain WORKING and evolve as the story gives us evidence.
