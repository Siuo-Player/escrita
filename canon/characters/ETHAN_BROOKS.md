# Protagonist — Ethan Brooks

Status: WORKING / established from project decisions
Updated: 2026-10-07

## Identity

**Name:** Ethan Brooks
**Age at entry:** 13
**Starting period:** approximately two weeks after the beginning of the school semester, before
the *Modern Family* pilot.
**School position:** one school year above Alex Dunphy.
**Sex/gender:** male.

Ethan is an American teenager from an affluent, educated family. The family has enough money to
support books, equipment, sport, martial-arts training, tutoring/resources when useful, and a
comfortable house, but they are not written as billionaire-level wealthy.

## Reincarnation

Ethan's previous life ends and he is reincarnated directly into a thirteen-year-old life in the
*Modern Family* world.

This avoids a timeline contradiction: the previous-life memories of *Modern Family* exist before
his reincarnated life begins in this world, even though the television series itself has not yet
started from the world's perspective.

The reincarnation is not a replacement of an existing canon child. Ethan is the biological son of
his own parents in this world, with an established childhood and family history. His previous-life
memories coexist with the memories and relationships of his current life.

The supernatural event itself is not the protagonist's public identity. To everyone else he is
simply Ethan Brooks, a normal thirteen-year-old who has moved to California.

## Family and origin

Ethan was born in the Seattle-area technology corridor and grew up in a financially comfortable,
academically supportive household.

### Father — David Brooks

David is a senior software/technology executive and early co-founder of a successful private
software company. He is technically capable, pragmatic, financially comfortable, and accustomed
to thinking several years ahead.

His career is the main reason the family can afford substantial educational resources without making
Ethan's life effortless.

### Mother — Melissa Brooks

Melissa works in a demanding professional career and is equally invested in education, discipline,
and giving Ethan opportunities without micromanaging him.

She is less technically oriented than David and provides a useful counterweight: Ethan's ability
should not become the entire family's identity.

### Move to Los Angeles

The family moves from the Seattle area to Southern California because David accepts a major
California-based leadership responsibility connected to his company.

The move happens shortly before Ethan's new school term. Ethan enters a new school environment
roughly two weeks into the term and is placed one school year above Alex Dunphy.

This gives the move an ordinary, believable cause and naturally puts Ethan into the same geographic
orbit as the Dunphys without making the Dunphys responsible for his arrival.

The family chooses a comfortable home in the same broad suburban area as the Dunphys. Proximity is
close enough for repeated natural encounters in the neighborhood and city, but Ethan does not begin
as a family member or instant best friend.

## Personality

Ethan's personality is strongly shaped by his established flaws.

He is intelligent, observant, cautious and capable of long-term planning, but he is also shy and
uncomfortable initiating conversations, especially with people he does not know.

His previous-life memories give him noticeably more maturity in planning and risk assessment than a
typical thirteen-year-old, but they do not remove adolescence.

His body is still thirteen:
- puberty affects mood and emotional regulation;
- attraction and embarrassment can override rational planning;
- social rejection still hurts;
- he can become self-conscious;
- he can make impulsive decisions despite knowing better.

The result should be a teenager with unusually good planning ability, not a forty-year-old mind
wearing a child's body.

## Academic ability

Ethan naturally performs at approximately an A-student level even without extensive studying.

With deliberate study, strong motivation, access to books/resources and his previous-life knowledge,
he can build a knowledge advantage equivalent to roughly **four additional years of structured study**
in subjects he has deliberately pursued.

This is not universal genius.

### Strong areas

- mathematics and logical reasoning;
- computing and technology;
- programming;
- technical self-study;
- systems thinking;
- physical training.

### Weak area

Ethan understands very little about the arts compared with his technical knowledge. Music, drawing,
painting, art history and related subjects do not suddenly become easy because he is intelligent.

This asymmetry is intentional. He should have areas where other people know more than him.

## Physical ability

Ethan has trained consistently since childhood.

Core abilities:
- strength;
- speed;
- flexibility;
- football/soccer;
- martial arts.

He is unusually well trained for thirteen, but remains physically constrained by age. He is not an
adult athlete, and training does not make him unbeatable.

He has experience as a **football/soccer captain**, giving him a social identity that exists independently
of the Dunphy family and a natural way to interact with peers after moving schools.

## Technology ambition

Ethan deliberately wants to study technology because he knows the field will become increasingly
important.

Technology is his first independent life engine.

He should build this through:
- sustained study;
- experiments;
- programming;
- books;
- projects;
- finding mentors and collaborators;
- gradually understanding what is commercially and technically possible in the period.

His future knowledge gives him direction, but not finished products.

He cannot simply "invent 2025 technology in 2009" without:
- understanding the underlying engineering;
- hardware/resources;
- money;
- collaborators;
- time;
- realistic constraints.

His parents can fund opportunities, but money cannot substitute for competence or years of work.

## Knowledge of Modern Family

Ethan retains an eidetic memory of everything that was actually shown in *Modern Family* in his
previous life.

This includes seemingly insignificant details and exact dialogue when he chooses to recall it.

Eidetic memory is not automatic omniscience.

He must actively retrieve a memory:
- a scene;
- a line;
- a sequence of events;
- a character habit;
- a location;
- a future consequence.

Therefore he can still fail to think of the relevant memory at the moment it matters.

He does **not** automatically know:
- private thoughts;
- events that occurred completely outside the show's presented material;
- information never revealed by the series;
- the private future of his own current life.

## Future memories beyond the series

Ethan also carries fragmented memories of future-world facts/events from his previous life.

These are:
- incomplete;
- non-personal;
- not a reliable encyclopedia;
- secondary to his *Modern Family* knowledge.

He does not remember his own future because this is a new life.

Fragments may be enough to recognize a technological trend, cultural shift, product category, historical
event, or likely direction, but they should not turn into perfect prediction.

## Relationship with Modern Family

Before the pilot, Ethan first encounters Alex at school.

Because his family has moved into the same broad area, he later encounters the other family members
naturally through:
- the neighborhood;
- school connections;
- sport;
- shops and public places;
- ordinary social events.

He does not begin with a magical reason to know the whole family intimately.

The early story should allow relationships to form at human speed.

## Narrative role

Ethan is not a camera attached to the Dunphys.

His life must contain meaningful events that would still exist if the Pritchetts, Dunphys and Tuckers
were absent:
- school;
- football;
- training;
- technology projects;
- friendships;
- family life;
- personal embarrassment;
- ordinary enjoyment;
- failures.

Modern Family then intersects with that life and creates new consequences.

The long-term test is:

**Could Ethan still have an interesting week if no canon character appeared?**

And the complementary test is:

**When Ethan enters a canon situation, does his presence actually change something?**

Both answers should eventually be yes.

## Constraints

Do not make Ethan:
- universally brilliant;
- socially fearless;
- artistically talented by default;
- physically unbeatable;
- financially all-powerful;
- perfectly predictive;
- emotionally detached from adolescence.

His strengths should create opportunities; his weaknesses should create problems.
