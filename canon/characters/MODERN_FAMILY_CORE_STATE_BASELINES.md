# Modern Family — Core Character State Baselines

Status: WORKING CONTINUITY REFERENCE — canon facts and AU additions are kept separate  
Baseline window: September–October 2009  
Updated: 2026-10-13

## Purpose and rules

This file supplies the **starting state** for the canon characters who may appear in the opening two episodes. It is not a substitute for checking the corresponding episode of *Modern Family*. The show remains the authority for dialogue, relationships, traits, possessions, and established events. Where the show does not establish a fact, mark it UNKNOWN or label it WORKING; do not turn an invented convenience into show canon.

Use these profiles compositionally with the dated scene ledger. A character's state at a scene equals:
1. this baseline;
2. show-canon events that have already happened by the scene date;
3. any established AU interaction or consequence dated before the scene;
4. the character's own prior-scene state changes.

**Knowledge rule:** before the Callahans move in, the Dunphy/Pritchett/Tucker households have no special knowledge of Nathan. No one knows about reincarnation, his private viewings of the show, or his attraction to Alex unless a later on-page event gives them evidence. Nathan's knowledge of canon is not knowledge the canon characters possess about him.

## Dunphy household

### Claire Dunphy — parent, organizer, and high-standard problem-solver

**Canon baseline:** Phil's wife; mother of Haley, Alex, and Luke; Jay's daughter and Mitchell's sister. During Season 1 she is a stay-at-home parent, not yet working at Jay's company. She values competence, preparation, and family responsibility; under stress, she can become controlling or competitive because she is trying to prevent foreseeable mistakes. She has a complicated relationship with Jay and with the standards she feels she must meet.

**Causal pattern:** Claire often notices a practical problem before others do. Her first response is to organize, correct, or take control; if that fails, the emotional reason underneath becomes visible. She is capable of warmth and humor, but concern can sound like criticism when she is tired. She should not be written as an all-knowing mother who instantly diagnoses someone else's private feelings.

**Ordinary life at the opening:** school logistics, groceries, household tasks, errands, planning, and the changing needs of three children at different stages. Her independent concerns continue when Nathan is not nearby. Her exact minute-by-minute schedule is in the recurring-schedules reference.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:**  
- Through 13 September: no established personal knowledge of the Callahans.  
- 14 September: meets the new neighbors during the move-day introduction if the selected Scene 26 beat is retained. She knows only the names and ordinary facts actually exchanged.  
- By 30 September: she can recognize Nathan as a nearby family’s son and as Alex’s school acquaintance. She does not know what Nathan privately thinks about Alex.  
- 1–15 October: any knowledge of Alex’s interactions must come from what Alex says or what Claire directly sees; no unexplained parental omniscience.

**Scene controls:** don't invent a workplace colleague for her; don't make her a regular employee at Jay's company this early; don't make her discuss a private family argument with Nathan without a plausible reason. If she notices an issue, record the visible clue that made her notice.

### Phil Dunphy — father, realtor, and connection-seeker

**Canon baseline:** Claire's husband and father of Haley, Alex, and Luke. He works in real estate, is enthusiastic about being a fun and involved dad, enjoys novelty and showmanship, and often uses humor or playful demonstrations to connect. He can misjudge how cool he is, but is also affectionate, observant in his own way, and genuinely committed to his family.

**Causal pattern:** Phil tends to approach people through friendliness, jokes, demonstrations, or offers of help. A joke is often an attempt to reduce awkwardness, not evidence that he understands the whole situation. When family pressure rises, he may initially try to lighten it before becoming more serious. Do not make every interaction a failed joke.

**Ordinary life at the opening:** real-estate appointments, client calls, family logistics, hobbies, and trying to participate in the children's lives. The story can show him between those activities without turning him into a device to introduce Nathan.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:**  
- Through 13 September: no personal knowledge of Nathan.  
- 14 September: introduces himself to the new neighbors during the move-day encounter, if used. If he helps carry a box, record which box and why he is present. He learns only the names offered in that exchange.  
- By 30 September: recognizes Nathan as the neighbor's son. No knowledge of Nathan's private memories or of Alex's unspoken reactions.  
- 14 October: if the family is dealing with the effects of DeDe's visit, Phil's mood can be affected, but Nathan cannot know the cause without a conversation or a visible clue.

### Haley Dunphy — high-school student, social life, and growing independence

**Canon baseline:** oldest Dunphy child and a high-school sophomore at the opening. Her immediate priorities often include friends, dating, image, and independence from Claire's rules. She can be self-focused or evasive, but she is not devoid of affection or practical judgment. Her conflict with Claire is partly a fight over control and partly an age-appropriate push for autonomy.

**Causal pattern:** Haley tends to judge social risk quickly, often using what her friends will think as a reference point. She may avoid a difficult conversation if an easier answer preserves her freedom, but her behavior should still have a reason. Her friends have their own schedules and disagreements; they are not a chorus whose only purpose is to approve Haley.

**Ordinary life at the opening:** high-school classes, school assignments, calls or texts appropriate to 2009, household rules, and plans with her existing friends. Do not give her present-day apps, slang, or smartphone behavior without checking 2009 plausibility.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** she has no established direct interaction with Nathan in the current opening outline. Being Alex's brother's new school acquaintance does not automatically make him part of Haley's social life. If she learns his name, sees his game, or speaks to him, record the actual cause and date first.

### Alex Dunphy — academically competitive middle-school student

**Canon baseline:** middle child; Haley's younger sister; Luke's older sister. She is academically exceptional, competitive about school, observant, and sometimes impatient with what she sees as irrational behavior. Her cello is an established activity. Her desire to be taken seriously can make social vulnerability uncomfortable; competence is both a strength and a defense.

**Causal pattern:** Alex notices evidence and inconsistencies. She may tease someone when she feels comfortable, but should not instantly understand a person's whole psychology. Her judgments are interpretations, not objective narratorial truth. She can be curious and kind without being romantically interested. A personal observation becomes justified only after enough visible behavior.

**Ordinary life at the opening:** seventh-grade classes, homework and studying, cello lessons/practice, family friction, and her own goals. Nathan being in eighth grade means that most core classes are separate, so encounters should use plausible shared locations: hallway, library, school resource, common activity, or a deliberate approach.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:**  
- Before 16 September: no established knowledge of him.  
- 16 September: sees the transfer student at school; unless they exchange words, she doesn't yet know his character or motives.  
- 18 September: first meaningful practical conversation over a school-library resource. After the introduction, she knows his name and that he is polite, capable, and somewhat self-conscious in that moment. She does not know he recognizes her from the show.  
- 21–22 September: small greetings/conversations give her more examples of his habit of backing away from praise or hedging an answer. The first remark about over-apologizing must be supported by those earlier examples.  
- 24–30 September: she can be curious about his project and recognize a conversational pattern; she cannot infer his reincarnation or assume attraction.  
- 1–15 October: trust and ease increase through repeated exchanges. This remains friendship/interest under observation, not an automatic romance deadline.

**Scene controls:** do not write piano lessons; do not give her access to Nathan's thoughts; do not make her intuit private facts she has not witnessed or been told. Her cello practice and school obligations persist off-page.

### Luke Dunphy — younger sibling, playful experimenter

**Canon baseline:** youngest Dunphy child in 2009. Luke is imaginative, energetic, occasionally impulsive, and often interprets a situation more literally or practically than the adults expect. His bond with Phil includes playful complicity; he also has a normal sibling relationship with Haley and Alex.

**Causal pattern:** Luke may act before planning every consequence, but his choices should arise from curiosity, play, frustration, or a concrete goal. He is not a universal source of random chaos and shouldn't know adult information merely because he is in the house.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no knowledge before the neighbor introduction. In the proposed 14 September scene, Luke may encounter Nathan at the driveway or with a football; if they exchange names or arrange a game, that specific event becomes the basis of later familiarity. He has no reason to know Nathan's school performance or relationship with Alex unless he learns it later.

## Pritchett household

### Jay Pritchett — business owner, husband, father, and grandfather

**Canon baseline:** Claire and Mitchell's father; Gloria's husband; Manny's stepfather; grandfather to the Dunphy children. He owns a successful closet/blinds business. Jay is practical, proud, and accustomed to having control. He can be emotionally guarded and make assumptions about family members, but his actions often show loyalty and care more clearly than his words.

**Causal pattern:** Jay values competence, independence, and getting things done. He can respond defensively when he feels disrespected, particularly around family or age, but should not be written as simply cold. His business concerns and family relationships continue independently of Nathan.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan during Episodes 1–2. The family plots on the Pilot date, 7 October's canon episode, and 14 October's canon episode remain parallel unless an actual encounter gives Nathan access. Jay does not know what Nathan remembers about the show.

### Gloria Pritchett — mother, wife, and protective family center

**Canon baseline:** Colombian-born; Jay's wife and Manny's mother. Gloria is expressive, socially confident, affectionate, and strongly protective of Manny and her family. Her speech and reactions reflect her own experiences; avoid using accent or cultural background as a generic punchline.

**Causal pattern:** Gloria tends to engage directly and emotionally. She may interpret a perceived slight personally, but has her own sense of humor, values, and practical intelligence. Do not infer her knowledge from scenes Nathan watched: the real Gloria can respond differently once the AU changes circumstances.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan during the opening two episodes. Any knowledge about him must originate in an actual meeting or someone telling her.

### Manny Delgado — child, reader, and unusually earnest observer

**Canon baseline:** Gloria's son and Jay's stepson. Manny is a child with unusually mature interests and mannerisms, romantic ideals, and a strong desire to be taken seriously. He is still ten/eleven-year-old in practical ability and dependence even when his speech sounds adult.

**Causal pattern:** Manny's behavior should arise from a genuine wish for romance, dignity, artistic/cultural interest, or belonging. His verbal maturity does not give him adult experience or adult control over events. He has school, family, and peer life outside Jay's household scenes.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan in Episodes 1–2.

## Tucker-Pritchett household

### Mitchell Pritchett — lawyer, son, brother, and father

**Canon baseline:** Jay's son and Claire's brother; Cameron's partner and Lily's father. Mitchell is a lawyer, precise and often anxious about social judgment. He can be restrained or controlling when he feels exposed, but he is also loving and capable of dry humor. Family tension with Jay is longstanding rather than created by Nathan.

**Causal pattern:** Mitchell frequently anticipates embarrassment and tries to manage appearances. He often becomes more candid when he feels safe, but not instantly. His work and parenting obligations constrain when he can appear.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan in Episodes 1–2. On 23 September and 14 October, show-canon events can affect him independently; Nathan is not inserted as an unexplained witness.

### Cameron Tucker — expressive partner, father, and social performer

**Canon baseline:** Mitchell's partner and Lily's father. Cameron is expressive, theatrical, sociable, and emotionally demonstrative. His performance or hosting instincts often intersect with real affection. He may escalate a story or event to make it memorable, but he is also attentive to those he loves.

**Causal pattern:** Cameron's dramatic behavior should have a social or emotional goal: to connect, entertain, protect, or regain attention. Do not flatten every reaction into theatrical overstatement. His parenting and relationship with Mitchell continue off-page.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan during Episodes 1–2.

### Lily Tucker-Pritchett — infant and caregiving constraint

**Canon baseline:** Mitchell and Cameron's adopted infant daughter. At this stage she is too young to carry a dialogue-driven independent plot; her needs affect adults' schedules, sleep, attention, and plans.

**Causal pattern:** when Lily appears, her presence must be consistent with who is caring for her, feeding/rest routines, and the household's physical arrangements. Do not place her in a scene as an inert prop or allow adults to leave her unattended just to facilitate an encounter.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no knowledge of Nathan and no direct relationship in the opening two episodes.

## DeDe Pritchett — visiting source of old family tension

**Canon baseline:** Jay's former wife and Claire/Mitchell's mother. Her relationships with the adult children and Jay contain longstanding history and unresolved resentment. The 14 October story thread must preserve the reasons and history shown by the source episode; do not invent Nathan as the cause of her visit or behavior.

**Nathan knowledge timeline:** no established contact with Nathan in the current outline. Unless a scene explicitly establishes an introduction, she does not know him and he should not be present during private family fallout merely because he recognizes the episode.

## Canon-versus-AU guardrails

- Nathan's memory may help him predict an initial tendency, but a prediction is not a fact about a character's current state.
- Record a divergence as soon as a canon character receives new information, meets Nathan, or makes a choice that differs from the source trajectory.
- Do not treat off-screen canon scenes as information the narrator can freely expose from Nathan's viewpoint. A parallel cut may show them; Nathan himself cannot know them without a plausible channel.
- For any character who gains meaningful dialogue or causes a scene outcome, add the exact-date snapshot in the relevant scene-state ledger. This file is the baseline, not a claim that the individual scene has already passed its continuity gate.
