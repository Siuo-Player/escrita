# Nathan Callahan — Knowledge and Skill Ledger

Status: WORKING OPERATIONAL REFERENCE; internal levels are not shown in the novel
Baseline date: 7 September 2009
Updated: 2026-10-12

This document is the writer's memory of what Nathan can know, do, remember, misunderstand and retrieve at each point in the story. It exists because a capability already acquired should not vanish merely because the next scene is easier to write without it.

Numeric levels below are provisional craft estimates, not measurements, RPG statistics or language to use in narration. Nathan's previous-life memories give him unusual advantages, but do not remove age, tools, time, incomplete knowledge, human error or social constraints.

## 1. Internal skill scale

| Level | Internal meaning | Writing implication |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | No established exposure / cannot perform | Do not let him use it without an origin or learning scene. |
| 1 | Recognition / basic familiarity | He may identify terms or know what to look up, but cannot reliably perform the task. |
| 2 | Beginner or limited practical competence | He can perform simple tasks, often with errors, references or guidance. |
| 3 | Independent functional competence | He can handle normal tasks and troubleshoot familiar problems; difficult edge cases remain difficult. |
| 4 | Strong/advanced for age and context | He can solve nontrivial problems, explain reasoning and combine techniques, but still needs time and can fail. |
| 5 | Exceptional / near-expert in the stated bounded domain | Use only where the profile and lived practice justify it. It is never omniscience, perfect performance or mastery of every related field. |

Use separate dimensions when useful: factual knowledge, practical execution, explanation, retrieval under pressure, confidence and willingness to use the capability. Knowing a concept does not guarantee being able to implement it under a deadline.

## 3. Knowledge classes — keep them separate

### A. Current-life factual knowledge

This includes everything Nathan learned in his present childhood through school, family, reading, sports, practice and ordinary experience. If a skill was acquired through a dated lesson or repeated practice, it persists until time, injury, disuse or changed circumstances plausibly alter it.

### B. Previous-life memories of Modern Family

Nathan has exceptionally detailed memory of what the show actually put on screen in his previous life. When the relevant material is actively retrieved, he can recall seemingly trivial behavior and, at times, exact lines. That does not give him full knowledge of off-screen events, private conversations, exact motives or unshown medical facts.

Keep two confidence values separate:
- **Memory confidence:** how clearly he recalls the original scene.
- **Prediction confidence:** how likely the same event/reaction is after his presence changes the timeline.

When Nathan changes an interaction, downgrade predictions whose causal dependencies have changed. A perfect memory of a broadcast scene cannot guarantee a perfect forecast of the altered world. See [Foreknowledge and Intervention Policy](../narrative/FOREKNOWLEDGE_INTERVENTION_POLICY.md).

### C. Fragmented previous-life facts about the real future

Nathan remembers broad directional patterns, especially the future importance of AI and computing. Specific recollections may be partial, decontextualized or wrong. Before he relies on a concrete external fact, record:
- what he thinks he remembers;
- whether it is exact, approximate or an inference;
- whether he can verify it in 2009;
- what current-world tools and knowledge he actually has;
- what happens if the memory is false.

Do not turn a vague remembered trend into exact stock prices, unpublished algorithms, passwords, future private events or effortless engineering blueprints.

### D. Beliefs and interpretations that may be wrong

A character's belief is not an objective fact. Nathan can overestimate how much a canon event is predetermined, underestimate his own effect on people, misunderstand a compliment or assume a peer is indifferent because that explanation feels safer. Record important mistaken beliefs as beliefs, including their source and the evidence that may eventually change them.

## 4. Initial knowledge inventory and limits

### Computing / first game

- Nathan begins developing his first mobile game around the opening of Episode 1 and completes it during Episode 2, roughly by 15 October 2009 under the current outline.
- The game needs visible iteration: goals, flow/route movement for enemies, steering/behavior failures, tests, fixes and polish. A successful algorithmic idea does not mean the product is finished.
- The player's age, available hardware, operating systems, libraries and distribution channels remain period-correct. Any tool must be added to the tools/prop inventory and be available in 2009.
- The story should show decisions and tests instead of turning prose into a code dump. The internal project file remains the source of technical continuity.

### Soccer / movement

- Nathan has years of soccer and physical training and was captain of his Seattle team.
- He can use scanning, spacing, timing, passing and tactical communication without re-learning them in California.
- His local authority resets after the move: he is a new teammate, does not control selection and does not know the group's private shorthand.
- Strength/speed/flexibility/martial-arts skills should be used when relevant, not forced into every scene. If he chooses not to use a clearly better capability, the scene needs a credible motive or cost.

### School / general knowledge

- Nathan earns A-level grades without extensive study.
- Deliberate study, resources and previous-life knowledge can place him around four years ahead in chosen academic domains over time.
- Do not use "four years ahead" as a universal number attached to every subject or a reason each teacher is instantly impressed.
- Art, music and interpersonal prediction remain genuine limitations.

## 5. Knowledge that must not be assumed

Unless a later scene establishes it, the following are UNKNOWN, not yes/no answers:
- allergies, intolerances, medication or hidden medical conditions;
- exact dominant foot, soccer positions beyond the current working role, or any old injury;
- proficiency in driving, first aid, cooking, repair, instruments, drawing, languages or other practical tasks;
- exact details of future-world events that are not in a saved memory;
- facts about canon characters that happened off-screen;
- whether another person likes, trusts, fears or resents Nathan;
- how a new coach, teammate or teacher will react to his ability.

The story may later decide these details. Once decided, update this ledger and every affected scene rather than improvising differently on a later day.

## 7. Pre-draft activation checklist

Before writing any scene featuring Nathan:
1. Check this ledger and the current character profile.
2. Identify relevant capabilities and available tools.
3. Decide whether a capable Nathan would naturally use them.
4. If not, state the actual constraint: he does not know the fact, cannot recall it, lacks tools/time, faces a physical limitation, chooses a social cost, is emotionally compromised or makes an understandable mistake.
5. Preserve every established ability in the next scene unless the world gives it a cause to change.
6. After the scene, update skill level, knowledge state, project status and what other characters have observed.

Do not write the number or level in the novel. Let it inform actions, options considered, errors, confidence and the amount of explanation Nathan needs.
