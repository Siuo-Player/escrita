## Family and home life

Cory lives with his parents and a younger brother. His parents have busy workdays but make a point of keeping the household lively at dinner; family conversations often become playful arguments about who won a game, who forgot a chore, or who gets the shared console next. His brother copies Cory's celebrations and annoys him by pressing buttons or changing settings before Cory has finished. Cory complains about this, but usually lets the younger boy try again rather than excluding him completely.

A nearby cousin is one source of borrowed games and weekend gaming sessions, so Cory has tried more genres than his family's own collection would suggest. He still shares access rather than owning every new release; his game time depends on schoolwork, family plans and whether someone else is using the console. His family supports football but expects homework and household responsibilities to be handled before a long gaming session. Saturday travel must be planned with his parent, and a visit to Nathan's home requires an explicit check rather than being assumed after a match.

## Personality, goals and habits

Cory is energetic, confident in one-on-one situations, and quick to make a joke when a group feels awkward. He likes being known as someone who can beat a defender and make teammates laugh. His teasing tests rapport, but he sometimes misjudges whether a new person is ready for that level of familiarity. He is not mean by default: if a joke clearly lands badly, he may change the subject rather than offer an eloquent apology.

He plays with momentum. In football he likes committing to a dribble before the opening closes; the strength is initiative, and the weakness is occasionally acting before he has checked what his teammates are doing. The same habit shows up in games: he presses start quickly, tries moving immediately, and reads instructions only when something blocks him. He likes direct feedback, visible goals and a clear distinction between an action that worked and one that did not. A hidden condition or unclear objective frustrates him because he feels the game withheld information, not because he wants a more advanced explanation.

Cory is socially outgoing, but not infinitely perceptive. He can make a room feel easier while missing signs that someone wants quiet. He likes competition, yet does not need every game or drill to become a serious ranking. He can be impatient and still generous about giving a new player another attempt.

## Games and expected testing perspective

Cory is a casual action/racing/arcade player, especially when a game lets him experiment and retry quickly. He does not know programming, pathfinding, AI architecture or performance profiling. He cannot diagnose the difference between a shared navigation field and a destination-prediction model.

When he tests Nathan's prototype, he is likely to rush toward the obvious opening rather than study the screen first. His most useful feedback concerns the player's understanding of the immediate goal: if the player reaches the exit area and nothing happens, Cory may not know whether he missed an objective, whether the exit is locked, or whether the game failed to register the action. He will describe that confusion plainly and point to what he tried. Do not make him deliver a polished usability report or comment on a bug he did not reproduce.

## Relationship to the team and Nathan

Cory knows the California squad's shorthand and enjoys teasing players he has started to trust. Nathan is initially a newcomer, so Cory may joke with him without assuming they are friends. He evaluates Nathan by whether he listens, contributes to drills and can take a joke in context.

If Cory attends the 10 October private playability session, this is because Maxwell invited him and the family transport/permission is confirmed. He plays alongside Maxwell but may notice a different issue because he approaches the game faster and pays less attention to explanatory text. He can report an unclear objective/exit cue; Maxwell's feedback should remain about the movement/corridor situation he personally experiences.

## Knowledge and continuity boundaries

Cory only knows what he plays, sees, hears or is told. At the test he can know that Nathan made the game and that it has a player, guards, an objective and an exit. He does not know Nathan's private memories or technical design and should not suddenly become an AI expert because the game is interesting.

## Continuity rules

- Keep his family, gaming access and transport context consistent.
- Distinguish his on-field confidence from carelessness in every situation; he can learn from a specific result.
- Keep his feedback experiential and concrete. Confusing an objective indicator is a useful player observation, not proof that the AI is wrong.
- If he is given dialogue or a consequential action elsewhere, record the scene date and update what he knows afterward.
