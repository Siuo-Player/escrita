# Jordan Reyes — Recurring Football Peer

Status: WORKING CHARACTER PROPOSAL
Updated: 2026-10-12

## Narrative function

Jordan is a recurring original character who gives Nathan a peer relationship outside the Dunphy family and outside his developing friendship with Alex. He is the competitive teammate already suggested in the Season 1 outlines; naming and stabilizing him should make those beats cumulative instead of inventing a new classmate whenever the plot needs a friend.

He is a middle-school football player in Nathan's new Southern California environment, around Nathan's age. He is not introduced as a rival who must be defeated, a bully, or a best friend after one match.

## Immediate objective

Jordan wants more meaningful minutes and a clearer role on the team. He has worked for his place and does not want either pity or a transfer student to make him irrelevant. His concern is ordinary and understandable; he is not secretly jealous of every achievement Nathan has.

He has his own tactical instincts and can make good decisions under pressure. Nathan's arrival unsettles him partly because coaches pay attention to the new player, but Jordan is capable of revising his opinion when Nathan's actions show that he is willing to share knowledge and help the team succeed.

## Relationship with Nathan

- **First appearance:** a Saturday football activity/match around 19 September 2009, after the move. Nathan has not earned a captaincy in the new team and does not assume leadership.
- **Early dynamic:** Jordan tests whether Nathan listens and makes useful decisions, rather than asking him to explain his whole background. Nathan notices Jordan's desire for playing time and offers a tactical suggestion only when it is relevant.
- **First shift:** a later training or match situation gives Jordan a chance to use the advice himself. The result belongs to Jordan; Nathan should not take the credit.
- **Friendship beat:** around the 9–10 October match, Jordan invites Nathan to do something after football. The invitation should be casual and specific, not a speech about becoming friends.
- **Longer-term use:** Jordan can disagree with Nathan, share team gossip, have his own training setbacks and sometimes be unavailable. He should not become a permanent audience for Nathan's genius.

## Voice and behavior

Jordan is direct, concrete and lightly competitive. He may tease after trust has developed, but should not use a signature catchphrase or constant insults. When he is annoyed, he tends to ask a practical question or go quiet rather than deliver a polished confrontation. When he is pleased, he is more likely to include Nathan in the next activity than explain how much he respects him.

His language should sound like an ordinary thirteen-year-old teammate, not a sports film coach. Avoid generic speeches about teamwork, sudden declarations that Nathan is special, or lines whose only purpose is to validate the protagonist.

## Continuity rules

- Keep his role and minutes coherent across matches.
- Do not promote him to captain or change his team status without an on-page cause.
- The new friendship must develop alongside Nathan's independent academic/technical life and his friendship with Alex.
- Give Jordan at least one goal, decision or problem in later outlines that has nothing to do with Nathan.
- This is a working proposal. Do not invent a detailed home life, exact birthday, ethnicity-specific traits or long-term fate until the story needs them.
