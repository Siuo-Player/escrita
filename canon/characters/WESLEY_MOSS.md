## Family and home

His mother, Elaine, handles scheduling and paperwork carefully and works in an administrative role at a local public utility. His father, Mark, is a maintenance technician for a municipal facilities contractor; his shifts can start early, but he is usually able to coordinate an evening ride when the family has notice. The household has one shared family car, so lifts depend on who has it and when.

Rachel's homework sometimes takes priority, and Wesley does not have a personal laptop or a collection of test devices. This is why he uses school-library machines for some work and why Nathan's invitation to try Current is a specific opportunity, not something Wesley could do at home. His parents expect him to ask permission and confirm pickup times rather than simply announce that he will be elsewhere.

The family has no special connection to software companies, game studios, Apple or the Callahans. His parents can arrange the 15 October visit once they know the time, but they do not provide Nathan with equipment, subscriptions, professional contacts or hidden expertise.

## Personality and habits

Wesley is patient, observant and a little reserved in unfamiliar social settings. He usually opens a conversation by asking about the task at hand; once a shared activity gives him a reason to speak, he becomes more conversational. He dislikes being treated as an expert simply because he is comfortable using a computer. He would rather say "I don't know" than bluff, and he usually points at the exact thing that confused him.

He likes puzzles, small strategy games and solving practical problems with clear constraints. He reads directions more carefully than Cory McBride, but he is not especially fast at reacting to action-game pressure. He prefers to understand what a game expects and then repeat a short section to see whether the result is consistent. If a control or visual state is unclear, he is likely to pause and try it again; he may describe an issue as "I couldn't tell whether it registered" or "I wasn't sure what changed." He will not name an algorithm, infer implementation defects from one symptom, or give a professional usability report.

He keeps a small spiral notebook for homework reminders and troubleshooting steps, labels computer files sensibly, and likes restoring order after a shared device gets left in a confusing state. He is not perfectionistic about every activity; he is methodical when the goal is unclear and relaxed when he understands the rules. He sometimes overestimates how much context another person needs before he can give useful feedback, so Nathan may need to ask one specific follow-up rather than expect a polished explanation.

## Games and technical ability

Wesley's experience comes from using a shared Windows desktop, school computers and games played at friends' houses. He can distinguish a game that is unclear from one that is difficult, but may need to repeat an attempt to make that distinction. He has no iPhone development environment, no iOS signing knowledge, and no ability to inspect Nathan's C++ source or diagnose the AI architecture.

Before 15 October, he has not tested Current on the iPod touch. If he tries a build, he only knows the controls and mechanics Nathan explains or that he discovers by playing. He can report a visible result—guards behaving differently, an objective state changing, whether the game responds to input—but not infer the code that produced it.
