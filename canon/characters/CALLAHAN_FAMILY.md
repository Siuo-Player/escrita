# Callahan family baseline

Status: WORKING
Purpose: stable background for continuity; family life is supportive context, not a main plot engine.
Updated: 2026-10-12

**Surname status:** Callahan is the author-confirmed shared family surname as of 2026-10-12. Nathan's given name was already locked; the author selected Callahan from the generated shortlist. See `canon/DECISION_LOG.md` and `canon/characters/CHARACTER_NAME_REGISTRY.md` for the decision trail. Clifford and Heather remain working given names selected by the logged random process.

## Nathan Callahan

Nathan is the only child for now. This is a working choice intended to keep the early family dynamic
focused; it can be revised before sibling-dependent plots are written.

## Clifford Callahan

Age at Nathan's entry: early-to-mid 40s.

Profession:
- software engineer by training;
- senior technology executive;
- early co-founder of a successful private software company;
- experienced enough to think several years ahead but still actively involved in technical decisions.

Financial history:
- the company became successful before Nathan's teenage years;
- Clifford has substantial equity and executive income;
- the family is affluent, not billionaire-level wealthy;
- money is available for education, equipment, sports, travel and projects, but purchases do not remove
  technical or social constraints.

Personality:
- pragmatic;
- organized;
- future-oriented;
- supportive of Nathan's curiosity;
- expects effort when Nathan commits to something;
- does not automatically believe that being intelligent makes an idea good.

Relationship with Nathan:
Clifford is proud of Nathan's academic ability but does not define him entirely by grades.
He is willing to buy books, computers, software, training and educational resources when Nathan can
explain why they are useful.

Move and career chronology:
Clifford is promoted into a Southern California regional technology/operations leadership role at the company he helped build. The role is approved on 11 September and takes effect on 14 September. The company’s relocation coordinator has been planning conditionally since the role was first discussed; the family does not make the final move until the offer, start date and relocation package are confirmed. The new job requires Clifford to handle handoffs and staffing decisions across time zones/teams, explaining longer hours without making him absent from every family scene.

The relocation package supplies a coordinator, an interstate moving company, a temporary furnished rental and a temporary vehicle. The move is organized around the family’s practical needs and school boundary, not around any knowledge of the Dunphys. The rental happens to be next door to them; neither the Callahans nor the relocation coordinator selected it because of the neighbors.

## Heather Callahan

Age at Nathan's entry: early-to-mid 40s.

Profession — working canon:
Heather is a senior contracts manager for a national healthcare-supply company. Her work involves reviewing supplier terms, resolving delivery/contract disputes, coordinating with procurement and operations, and keeping a paper trail precise enough that colleagues can act on it. She is organized and professionally capable without being a software engineer; she does not casually debug Nathan's code or understand every game subsystem.

Her employer has a Southern California regional office. Heather requests an internal transfer once Clifford's role becomes definite; the transfer is approved for a start date of Monday, 21 September 2009. She uses previously accrued leave and relocation days from 14–18 September to move and prepare for the new school routine. She does a small amount of transition email/call work when necessary, but she is not expected to work a full office day while travelling or unpacking. Until 21 September, do not write her as attending a California office; from then on, weekday availability must respect normal business hours and commute.

This transfer is a continuity decision to make the relocation's effect on her career explicit. It does not become a separate employment subplot unless a later event creates a reason for one.

Personality:
- practical;
- academically supportive;
- attentive to Nathan's emotional state;
- less interested in technical detail than Clifford;
- willing to challenge Nathan when perfectionism or overplanning becomes unhealthy.

Relationship with Nathan:
Heather protects the distinction between "being capable" and "having to be perfect".
She supports his education and training while expecting him to maintain ordinary responsibilities.

## Move to Southern California — operational continuity record

### Why this rental

The employer's relocation coordinator shortlists temporary houses based on a ready-to-occupy lease, a reasonable commute to Clifford's new office, safe and ordinary family housing, and an address that keeps Nathan within the target middle-school boundary. A local agent confirms availability from the corporate-housing inventory. The Callahans accept the available house for those practical reasons. They have no previous relationship with Phil, Claire, Luke, Alex or Haley and no advance knowledge of the neighboring household. The next-door location is coincidence, not a plan.

The temporary lease starts on 14 September and is not a purchase or a promise that the family will remain in that neighborhood permanently. Basic utilities and internet service are active before the family arrives; this lets Clifford make a short remote handoff call after arrival without pretending he has begun a normal office day.

### Travel, cargo and personal equipment

- **12 September:** movers pack the Seattle home and inventory boxed property. The moving truck takes the household goods, books, larger monitor/peripherals, kitchen items and furniture onto the interstate route. The family keeps a written inventory and the essentials needed for the final Seattle days.
- **13 September:** the moving crew travels south. This is a logistics day, not a family scene and not an extra day in California.
- **14 September:** the family takes a morning flight from Seattle to Los Angeles, collects the relocation-provided temporary vehicle at the airport, and drives to the rental late in the morning. Movers arrive in the early afternoon and unload through the afternoon. Some rooms remain blocked by boxes; the house is habitable but far from settled.
- Nathan keeps the Intel MacBook, charger, compact input peripherals, essential notes and second-generation iPod touch with him in personal luggage. The MacBook already has the period-appropriate development environment and current project copy. This is why he can open the project at the rental even before the main boxes are unpacked. The moving company carries bulky, nonessential equipment; never make Nathan wait for a boxed desktop or monitor before he can continue coding.
- Clifford keeps his company laptop, credentials and work papers with him. He makes a short remote transition/handoff call from the rental only after the family arrives and the internet connection is available. The call is not equivalent to a full day physically working in the new office.
- The family car is transported separately and arrives later that week; until it is delivered, they use the relocation-provided temporary vehicle. Do not assume both cars and all household goods are available at the rental on arrival.

### School transfer

The target school is identified provisionally while the move is being planned. The relocation coordinator helps the family establish that the rental address falls within the intended school's attendance area. Clifford and Heather contact the school office before departure and send the initial transfer paperwork; Nathan's previous school prepares his records for transfer. The signed temporary lease and the family's document folder travel with them.

On **15 September**, Heather completes the in-person enrollment/records check, provides the remaining address and health/immunization paperwork and confirms Nathan's timetable and first-day instructions. This is why Nathan can begin on **Wednesday, 16 September** despite the short move window. The exact fictional school's internal administrative process is not an on-page plot; the state fact is that enrollment is accepted and the necessary records have been requested/received before the first school day.

### What the family feels

The move is primarily a parental career decision. Nathan experiences it as a major personal transition: new school, new teammates, new neighborhood, loss of familiar routines and an opportunity to rebuild his social life. The family's financial position makes the logistics manageable but does not make the move emotionally easy.

This distinction is useful for early episodes: Nathan can have a genuinely difficult adjustment even
though his basic living conditions are good.

## Continuity constraints

Keep the following stable unless a later story decision explicitly changes them:
- Clifford's income/wealth comes from a technology career and equity, not unexplained inheritance.
- Heather has her own career.
- Neither parent knows about Nathan's previous life.
- Neither parent treats Nathan's future knowledge as ordinary teenage knowledge.
- Nathan's parents can fund opportunities but cannot give him adult experience, social confidence,
  research breakthroughs or instant competence.
