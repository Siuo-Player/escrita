This is association football (football/soccer), not American football. In Portuguese narration call it futebol; in the characters' U.S. dialogue, "soccer" is the natural period/location term unless a character has a reason to say otherwise.

## How to read this roster

The first roster belongs to Nathan's life before the move. The second belongs to his new Southern California school/community football environment. They are different teams; the Seattle teammates do not relocate with the Callahans.

The line-ups are a coherent first baseline for testing Episode 1–2 pacing, not permanent canon. Team roles, names, relationships and match details are WORKING unless explicitly author-locked. A later scene may alter selection, formation, minutes, injury status or relationships only if the cause is recorded and later scenes are reconciled.

Every named player below has a first-pass causal background: what he wants, what he is protective of, how he tends to act, and what his recent team context has been. Before any player has dialogue or affects a scene, expand the "current state at scene date" using the [Character State Continuity Standard](CHARACTER_STATE_CONTINUITY_STANDARD.md). A surname or jersey number is not a personality.

### Calendar/continuity guardrails

- Monday, 7 September 2009 is Labor Day in the U.S. Do not schedule a normal school day or regular team training that day. If Episode 1's birthday sequence includes football, frame it as Nathan's solo work, an informal kickabout or a family activity unless the outline deliberately changes.
- The current outline schedules the old school's first formal week beginning Wednesday, 9 September. The squad's ordinary practice pattern is Tuesday/Thursday and a match or team event on Saturday.
- The family move is expected around 14 September; the old team's goodbye context is around the 12 September activity/match. Keep the exact score, opponent, individual goal-scorers and any injury unset until the scene requires them.
- The new-team scene around 19 September is best treated as a controlled training game/scrimmage unless the outline establishes that player registration and competition rules permit an official fixture. That lets the coach assess Nathan without making him captain or guaranteeing his long-term starting place on day one.
- The age/date concern was reviewed and closed under DEC-012: Nathan turns thirteen on 7 September 2009, while the prior-life transition roughly two weeks earlier is a separate event. Do not reopen it as a contradiction.

## A. Seattle-area team — Nathan's old team

**Context:** a school/community middle-school side with a stable core and a coach who values simple positional discipline. It is a plausible local team for a gifted, well-trained young teenager; it is not a professional academy. Nathan is captain because teammates trust his positioning, communication and willingness to take responsibility, not because he is better at everything.

**Working formation:** 4-3-3. Numbers are references for this baseline, not permanent jersey assignments.

### Seattle coach

**Leonard Holloway — head coach, WORKING**

Leonard is a local physical-education teacher and community coach in his early forties. He played organized football through college but never built a professional career; he values coaching because he can help young players understand a game that once gave him structure. His team talks are brief and focus on positioning and effort, not inspirational speeches. He chose Nathan as captain after seeing him explain corrections without humiliating teammates and accept blame when his own pass caused a turnover. Leonard is pleased with Nathan's growth but has deliberately not built the whole team around one child. Before the 12 September activity he has to balance the squad's emotional reaction to the move with the need to prepare properly. His private life beyond the routine needed for this story is not yet specified; do not invent family obligations to explain his availability without adding them to his state record.

## C. Causal rules for football scenes

1. **Minutes are state.** Record who starts, substitutes, is unavailable and why for each match.
2. **Captaincy is not transferable.** Nathan's Seattle captaincy is established history. California has its own hierarchy.
3. **A coach remembers.** A coach who saw a mistake, correction or act of leadership may refer back to it later.
4. **Skill must show through choices.** Demonstrate scanning, spacing, tempo and movement, but do not give every teammate the same tactical vocabulary.
5. **An absence has a cause.** Illness, transport, family obligations, schoolwork, injury or selection can explain non-attendance; none should be invented retroactively after it becomes convenient.
6. **A medical blank is not a negative.** Allergies, injuries and health limits remain UNKNOWN until established.
7. **Scorelines and results remain unlocked** until the relevant scene is shaped. A random name draw does not randomize who wins.
