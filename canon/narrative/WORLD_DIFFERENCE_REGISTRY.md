# Parallel-World Difference Registry

Status: AUTHORITATIVE WORLD CANON — SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH  
Updated: 2026-11-19

This registry records the differences between the real world Nathan remembers from his previous life and the parallel world in which the story takes place. It is not a list of every fictional event: episode-level events and their consequences remain in EVENT_REGISTRY.md.

## 1. Authority and interpretation

1. **This registry defines the world's explicit divergences.** When a confirmed entry conflicts with a real-world source, the story follows the parallel-world rule. The source remains useful for understanding what was different in the real world.
2. **No recorded difference means no assumed difference.** Unless an entry below says otherwise, use the documented real-world baseline for geography, history, calendars, science, technology, law, institutions, economics and culture.
3. **Do not turn research uncertainty into a world difference.** A missing source, an unresolved legal question or an implausible draft detail is not permission to invent an alternate rule. Resolve it deliberately and record it first.
4. **Remembered episode titles are source-memory labels.** In the parallel world, the relevant events are lived events involving real people. Do not describe them as a current-world television broadcast, a scene being acted, or a scripted sitcom event. It is not yet established whether a television adaptation called *Modern Family* also exists in this world; do not assume that it does.
5. **The butterfly effect is cumulative.** The original series timeline is an initial reference for Nathan's memories, not a script that the parallel world must obey. Once a meaningful change alters timing, relationships, access or information, later predictions depending on it must be reconsidered. Do not silently reset the story to the original episode sequence.
6. **Use the registry IDs in dependent documents.** The hidden marker <!-- WD-001 --> (with the relevant ID) identifies a document governed by an entry. When a new divergence is approved, add an entry, mark the affected canon documents, update dependent outlines/drafts and add a regression check where a deterministic rule can be tested.

## 3. Real-world baseline invariants

The following are **not** currently alternate-world differences. They remain grounded in the real-world 2009 baseline unless a future WD entry explicitly changes them:

- **Geography:** Seattle, Los Angeles, Cheviot Hills, school districts, roads and neighbourhood layouts follow real-world geography.
- **Calendar and public chronology:** Gregorian dates, weekdays, school calendars, public holidays and documented historical events remain real-world reference points. The date borrowed from a remembered episode does not imply that the episode is being broadcast in the parallel world.
- **Technology:** iPhone 3GS hardware, iPhone OS, computers, programming languages, development tools and networking capabilities retain their researched period limitations. Nathan's fictional achievements do not change the underlying hardware specifications.
- **Law and institutions:** Apple developer programmes, contracts, school systems, property rules, tax rules and other institutions use the sourced real-world baseline unless a divergence is deliberately established.
- **Science and physical reality:** no different laws of physics, mathematics, medicine or biology have been established.
- **Culture and economics:** ordinary 2009 social, cultural and economic conditions remain the baseline unless the story explicitly records an exception.

A fictional achievement inside the story is not automatically a change to the world's underlying technology or laws. For example, Nathan writing an original game is a story event; it does not mean the iPhone 3GS has different hardware.

## 4. Open questions — do not silently decide

- Whether a television production called *Modern Family* also exists in the parallel world is not established. Until decided, it must not be treated as a current-world programme or broadcast.
- Nathan remembers what was actually shown in his previous life; do not infer complete knowledge of private, off-screen conversations, motives or events.
- The exact future events that will diverge are not pre-decided by this registry. Record concrete changes and their consequences in the event registry and relevant episode canon.
- Any additional changes to real-world technology, laws, public history, geography or institutions require an explicit new WD entry. Do not infer them from narrative convenience.

## 5. Change protocol

For every newly approved difference:

1. Assign the next unused WD-### identifier.
2. Record the real-world baseline and the exact parallel-world rule separately.
3. State scope, status, canon basis and dependent documents.
4. Add the hidden WD marker to every listed dependent document.
5. Update affected profiles, event records, outlines and drafts together.
6. Add a regression test for any deterministic, machine-checkable constraint.
7. Search the repository for older wording that now contradicts the new rule.
8. Preserve unresolved or retired decisions as history; do not silently rewrite them as if they had always been true.

Automated tests can validate IDs, document references and selected wording constraints. They cannot prove that every scene is semantically consistent; that still requires a deliberate continuity review.
