# Story scope and protagonist design

Status: WORKING
Trust: WORKING
Updated: 2026-10-07

## Core direction

This is an episodic Modern Family fanfic with a protagonist who has a life of his own.

Modern Family is a major social/world axis, not the entirety of the plot.

The protagonist should:
- build relationships with the Dunphy/Pritchett/Tucker orbit naturally;
- have goals, routines, interests, friendships, studies/work, and problems that exist outside the
  show's plots;
- sometimes enjoy ordinary life that has nothing to do with canon;
- occasionally pursue themes or subjects that open different kinds of stories;
- use future/show knowledge selectively and plausibly rather than as an omniscient cheat;
- sometimes get things wrong because memory is incomplete and the future can diverge;
- affect canon through choices and relationships, rather than by mechanically "fixing episodes".

## Balance target

Across a typical arc:
- some scenes should be primarily protagonist life;
- some should connect that life to Modern Family;
- some should be about other characters' own goals;
- only some should directly intersect a recognizable canon event.

There is no hard percentage requirement. The test is whether removing Modern Family canon
characters from a scene would make at least some scenes stop working, while other scenes would still
have a genuine purpose because they belong to the protagonist.

## Future knowledge

Use future knowledge as a character resource, not as the story's main engine.

Useful forms:
- remembering a likely future event;
- recognising a person's tendency;
- knowing that a decision may have consequences;
- preparing for a future opportunity;
- noticing a warning sign earlier than someone else would.

Avoid:
- listing future events for exposition;
- perfectly predicting everyone;
- using future knowledge to solve every conflict;
- changing canon simply because the protagonist "knows the script";
- making the protagonist morally detached because he thinks outcomes are predetermined.

When the timeline diverges, previously remembered knowledge should lose certainty.

## Character integrity

Canon characters may develop differently because circumstances changed, but their choices should
still emerge from recognizable motives, habits, fears, strengths, contradictions, and relationships.

Avoid changing a character's personality just to make the protagonist's preferred pairing or plot
easier.

## Episode test

Before drafting an episode, answer:

1. What does the protagonist want now?
2. Why does he want it?
3. What changes because he acts?
4. What can go wrong?
5. What does he learn or misunderstand?
6. Which relationship changes?
7. What remains true after the episode that was not true before?
8. Which parts are his own life, and which parts are a Modern Family intersection?

After drafting:

- Remove scenes that only restate canon.
- Keep scenes that reveal character, create causality, deepen a relationship, establish a theme, or
  create a later consequence.
- Prefer concrete scenes over summaries when a decision or emotional change matters.
- Use time skips only after the reader understands what changed.

## POV rule

Default POV should remain close to the protagonist.

Occasional secondary POVs are allowed when they reveal consequences, misunderstandings, or emotions
the protagonist genuinely cannot observe.

POV changes must earn their place; they are not decoration.
